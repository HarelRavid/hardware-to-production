## Listener promise
Compare manual, assisted, semi-automatic and automated alternatives using lifecycle cash flow, quality, capacity, maintenance, flexibility and uncertainty—not labor savings alone.

## Cold open
[ILLUSTRATIVE] A cell shows two-year payback on labor. Volume arrives lower, retooling is needed, downtime is higher, and the saved operators were reassigned rather than removed.

## Beat 1 — Define alternatives
Claims: EP42-C01
Include do-nothing/staged option.

## Beat 2 — Labor saving is not automatically cash saving
Claims: EP42-C02
Guard: operating model must actually change.

## Beat 3 — Full lifecycle boundary
Claims: EP42-C03
CapEx/NRE/integration/qualification/maintenance/spares/software/downtime/yield/changeover/change cost.

## Beat 4 — Reuse Wave 03 economics
Claims: EP42-C04
NPV/payback/break-even/sensitivity; no universal hurdle.

## Beat 5 — Capacity and quality assumptions must be evidence-linked
Nominal machine rate and assumed yield cannot overwrite Wave 02/03 evidence.

## Beat 6 — Staged CapEx
Claims: EP42-C05
Fixture/assisted/semi-auto now; larger automation later when evidence improves.

## Beat 7 — Downside case
Demand lower; ramp delay; maintenance higher; product revision/retooling.

## Listener tool — Automation Business Case Sheet
need → alternatives → demand → fixed investment → operating cost → quality/yield → accepted throughput → maintenance → change flexibility → risk/sensitivity → staged/full/defer.

## Misconceptions
- operator savings = cash savings;
- machine quote = total investment;
- best unit cost = best investment;
- forecast = evidence;
- short payback always better than NPV;
- sunk automation should dictate product design.

## Closing action
Re-run one automation business case with demand -25%, maintenance +50%, and one major product change. If the decision flips, those assumptions are engineering work.

## Handoff
EP43 asks how much of the operation should actually be automated.
