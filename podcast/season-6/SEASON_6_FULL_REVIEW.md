## 1. Season objective
Season 6 must convert the Hardware-to-Production system from a set of learned frameworks into a usable decision discipline.

The season should teach listeners to:
- reconstruct failures without hindsight or blame shortcuts;
- extract mechanisms from successful industrialization without survivorship mythology;
- make proportionate startup manufacturing decisions;
- borrow useful practices from mature industries without copying their applicability burden blindly;
- connect configuration, supplier/process state, rework, field evidence and lifecycle change;
- synthesize the full hardware journey through one integrated decision story.

Result: **PASS.**

5. create explicit expiration conditions for startup shortcuts;

## 6. Cross-industry transfer audit
The season consistently uses the following rule:

> Transfer the engineering mechanism; preserve the source industry's contractual, regulatory, economic and assurance boundaries.

### Automotive
Transfer:
linked planning/risk/control/measurement/approval, supplier evidence, genealogy.
Do not automatically transfer:
customer-specific PPAP/IATF obligations or high-volume overhead.

### Medical device
Transfer:
risk-linked process evidence, rework history, traceability, effectiveness evidence.
Do not automatically transfer:
FDA/QMSR/ISO jurisdictional obligations outside their scope.

### Aerospace
Transfer:
configuration-specific evidence, rework closure, mechanism-aware supplier/inspection control.
Do not automatically transfer:
aviation certification/FAI/assurance burden.

### Industrial equipment
Transfer:
installed-base identity, obsolescence planning, change-impact review, recommissioning and recovery.
Do not automatically transfer:
specific vendor architectures or jurisdiction-specific machinery obligations.

Result: **PASS.**

## 10. Narrative/editorial audit
The season avoids two major traps:

1. `famous failure = simple moral story`
2. `famous successful company = best-practice proof`

Recommended recurring language pattern during scripting:
- “The source documents…”
- “The company/regulator/investigator states…”
- “That supports this narrower lesson…”
- “It does not prove…”

This language should remain natural rather than legalistic, but source ownership must stay visible.

Result: **STRONG PRODUCTION ARCHITECTURE.**
