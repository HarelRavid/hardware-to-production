## Two-character tension
Rona:
“If GE and Toyota succeeded with these methods, why not copy them?”
Oz:
because success stories carry survivorship and context bias.

## Hook
GE’s LEAP nozzle was not simply the old part printed differently; GE says it was redesigned around additive manufacturing.

## Teaching flow
1. Why success cases require more causal humility than failure cases.
2. GE LEAP — product-process redesign; one-piece architecture; qualification/serial transition.
3. Company-reported benefits vs independently established mechanism.
4. GM + Ventec — complementary capabilities in emergency scale-up.
5. HHS/ASPE transfer boundary: scale/logistics vs medical product/regulatory domain.
6. Toyota/TPS only as supporting context, not universal formula.
7. Transfer check.
8. Practical action.

## Primary tool — Success Pattern Transfer Check
Observed practice
→ documented change
→ documented outcome
→ plausible mechanism
→ what else changed?
→ source attribution
→ volume/risk/regulatory/customer context
→ supplier/capex constraints
→ transfer limit
→ smallest safe experiment.

## Misconceptions
- successful company proves causal method;
- fewer parts is always better;
- additive is automatically cheaper;
- automotive scale knowledge can replace product/regulatory expertise;
- Toyota/TPS is a universal maturity endpoint.

## Closing action
Choose one admired manufacturing practice and document what evidence would justify transferring it to your context.
