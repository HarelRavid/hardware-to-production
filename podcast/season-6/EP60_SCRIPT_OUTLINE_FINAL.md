because the context, evidence, configuration, volume, supplier/process state and commitment change as the product matures.

## Hook
Return to the Sentinel Node at the very beginning: a small outdoor machine-monitoring sensor concept with incomplete requirements and no factory.

## Teaching flow
1. Idea → Requirements: make uncertainty explicit.
2. Architecture/interfaces: ownership and interaction claims.
3. POC: fastest credible learning; representativeness limits.
4. Integrated/engineering prototype: mechanical/electronics evidence and verification intent.
5. Production-intent hardware: process/supplier/test/configuration begin converging.
6. LVP: bridge routes, controlled manual work, genealogy, defects/rework.
7. Production validation: integrated production-system evidence.
8. Ramp: accepted throughput, moving constraint, supplier/capacity/economics.
9. SVP: sustained process/control/measurement/change/recovery.
10. Field: FIELD EVIDENCE LOOP and controlled change.
11. Replay decisions that changed as context changed.
12. Full Decision Ledger.
13. Series close.

## Misconceptions
- maturity is a linear checklist;
- once a decision is made it stays valid;
- production intent means final everything;
- field data arrives after engineering is over;
- tools matter more than the evidence question.

## Closing action
Take your current hardware product and write the five decisions whose evidence or expiration condition you cannot reconstruct today.

## Final series close
The goal is not “more manufacturing paperwork.”
It is to make hardware decisions explicit enough that the product can change, scale and recover without losing engineering truth.
