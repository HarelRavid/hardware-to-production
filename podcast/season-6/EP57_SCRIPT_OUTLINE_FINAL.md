## Two-character tension
Speaker B:
“If the recall fixes the original problem, why should the replacement process need its own qualification?”

Speaker A:
because remediation creates a new material/process/configuration state.

## Hook
Some reworked Philips ventilators were later recalled for problems introduced or remaining in the rework/remediation state.

## Teaching flow
1. Separate the 2021 broad Philips recall from later reworked-device populations.
2. Rework creates new as-built/evidence state.
3. Silicone-foam separation / residual PE-PUR example.
4. Serial-programming configuration issue.
5. Consent decree: independent testing + remediation plan + restricted production/sale under conditions.
6. Smith & Nephew packaging/seal case — packaging process can be product-critical evidence.
7. Philips Achieva adhesive/process-control example.
8. FDA QMSR current U.S. context.
9. Regulated-Industry Transfer Check.
10. Practical action.

## Primary tools
### Rework Evidence Card
Original state
→ failure/recall condition
→ remediation action
→ new material/process/configuration
→ affected population
→ new verification
→ field/effectiveness evidence
→ remaining uncertainty.

### Regulated-Industry Transfer Check
Source obligation/mechanism
→ jurisdiction/product/role
→ engineering mechanism
→ what transfers
→ what remains regulated/context-specific.

## Guards
- do not merge Philips populations/timelines;
- QMSR is U.S. context;
- consent decree applies to named parties/conditions;
- no exact ISO 13485 clause unless separately locked.

## Misconceptions
- rework restores old state;
- packaging is secondary to the device;
- corrected product automatically closes effectiveness;
- regulated-industry practice transfers as law to other sectors.

## Closing action
For one remediation/rework, write the new as-built state and what new evidence is required before trust is restored.
