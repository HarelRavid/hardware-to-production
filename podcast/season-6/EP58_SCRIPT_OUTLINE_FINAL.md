## Listener transformation
Before: “Aerospace quality is simply more inspection and more paperwork.”
After: “I can understand why approval, FAI, supplier audits, rework closure and lifecycle inspection remain bounded evidence—and borrow those mechanisms without copying aerospace assurance burden.”

## Two-character tension
Rona:
“How can a part pass FAI and audits and still have a manufacturing problem?”

Oz:
because approval only proves the characteristics/process evidence actually inside its demonstrated envelope.

## Hook
The 787 battery investigation documented FAI/audit evidence—and still identified manufacturing features the existing process/inspection system had not adequately recognized.

## Teaching flow
1. Approval evidence is bounded.
2. 787 battery — FAI/audit context vs hidden process features/inspection capability.
3. Supplier/subtier ownership and defect-detection capability.
4. 737-9 door plug — rework/removal/documentation/closure chain.
5. Rework as configuration transition.
6. United 328 PW4077 — manufacturing-created state + fatigue/lifecycle inspection interpretation.
7. IAQG 9102 context and applicability guard.
8. Aerospace Evidence Transfer Check.
9. Practical action.

## Guards
- do not say FAI/audits are ineffective generally;
- do not reduce 787 battery event to supplier quality only;
- use final NTSB wording for United 328 causal conclusion;
- exact P&W estimates attributed;
- AS9100 does not universally mandate 9102.

## Misconceptions
- passed FAI = process fully understood;
- completed audit = every mechanism controlled;
- more inspection always solves process risk;
- aerospace process should be copied unchanged into startup hardware.

## Closing action
Take one approval package and list the mechanisms it does not actually demonstrate or detect.
