# Episode 37 Production Blueprint — Make or Buy?

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 4 — Quality, Suppliers and the Reality of Scale
lifecycle: DEV → LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: frames make/buy mainly as piece-price comparison.
After: evaluates strategic control, process knowledge, IP, capability, capital, utilization, quality risk, supply risk, lead time, change velocity and total economics.

## Narrative hook
A team outsources a process because the supplier quote is cheaper. Months later every design change takes weeks, failure analysis is opaque and the team cannot tell whether the supplier process is capable. The cost decision quietly became a control decision.

## Teaching flow
1. Define the capability, not only the part.
2. Strategic/learning/IP importance.
3. Internal capability and utilization reality.
4. External supplier maturity and dependency.
5. Capital/NRE versus variable cost.
6. Quality, traceability and change-control burden.
7. Lead time and engineering iteration speed.
8. Single-source/geopolitical/logistics/resilience risk.
9. Hybrid models: develop internally, scale externally; dual-process strategies.
10. Revisit make/buy as volume and evidence mature.

## Core framework — Make/Buy Decision Model
`Capability/claim → strategic importance → internal maturity/capacity → supplier maturity → IP/learning → quality/evidence control → change speed → supply/resilience risk → CAPEX/NRE → cost per accepted good unit → decision → review trigger`.

## Listener tools
- Make/Buy Decision Card.
- Outsourcing Readiness Check: do we understand the process well enough to specify, verify and change it externally?

## Common mistakes
- cheapest quote wins;
- outsourcing a poorly understood process and expecting the supplier to define the requirement;
- ignoring utilization when justifying internal CAPEX;
- treating IP risk and engineering learning as non-economic;
- assuming outsourcing transfers accountability for product conformity.

## Source/evidence backlog
Financial examples require audited assumptions. Sector/customer restrictions on special processes, approved sources or domestic sourcing require exact applicability.

## Closing handoff
If the decision is to buy, Episode 38 asks how to determine whether a supplier truly has the required capability rather than merely a persuasive sales presentation.
