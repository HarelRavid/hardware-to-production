# Episode 38 Production Blueprint — Selecting a Supplier by Capability, Not by Sales Deck

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 4 — Quality, Suppliers and the Reality of Scale
lifecycle: LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: qualifies suppliers by certifications, references, samples and headline equipment lists.
After: evaluates whether the actual source can repeatedly make, measure, control, trace and change the required product/process.

## Narrative hook
A supplier has the right machines, ISO certificates and beautiful sample parts. Production starts and variation appears because the real issue was fixture strategy, measurement method, operator dependence and sub-tier control — none of which appeared in the sales deck.

## Teaching flow
1. Start from product/process claims and CTQs.
2. Equipment fit versus demonstrated process capability.
3. Material/source and sub-tier controls.
4. Measurement/test capability.
5. Tooling, maintenance and process control.
6. Engineering depth and failure-response capability.
7. Traceability, configuration and change discipline.
8. Capacity, load, continuity and recovery.
9. Sample evidence versus production evidence.
10. Supplier audit/assessment proportional to consequence and maturity.

## Core framework — Supplier Capability Assessment
`Requirement/CTQ → process/equipment → material/sub-tier → tooling → control method → measurement → capability evidence → operator dependency → genealogy → capacity → change control → recovery → gap/action`.

## Listener tools
- Supplier Capability Scorecard.
- Sample-to-Production Evidence Gap Review.

## Common mistakes
- certification = process capability;
- equipment list = demonstrated capability;
- three good samples = stable production source;
- audit checklist disconnected from actual CTQs;
- ignoring sub-tier suppliers and outsourced special processes;
- accepting uncontrolled supplier changes.

## Source/evidence backlog
Supplier-approval and audit requirements vary by customer/sector. Any mandated qualification, special-process approval or quality-system requirement must be exact-source/applicability controlled.
