## Cold open

[ILLUSTRATIVE]
Team says product is “ISO compliant,” but nobody can identify standard, edition, scope, applicable clauses or product evidence.

## Beat 1 — Authority classes
Claim: EP36-C01.
regulation / consensus standard / customer requirement / internal specification / guidance.

## Beat 2 — Standard vs law
Claim: EP36-C02.
ISO standards are voluntary unless made binding through applicable law/contract/etc.

## Beat 3 — Certification vs product evidence
Claim: EP36-C03.
QMS certification does not automatically prove every product/process claim.

## Beat 4 — Applicability
Claim: EP36-C04.
source / edition / status / product / process / market / site / supplier / contract / owner.

## Beat 5 — Edition/status control
Claim: EP36-C05.
Use current correction:
ISO 9001:2026 replaced 2015.
ISO 9000:2026 replaced 2015.

## Beat 6 — Standard embedded in regulation
Claim: EP36-C06.
FDA QMSR as scoped U.S. medical-device example only.

## Beat 7 — Requirement-to-evidence link
Claim: EP36-C07.

## Beat 8 — Source integrity
Claim: EP36-C08.
Paid normative text / exact clauses require verified access.

## Listener tool — Applicability & Evidence Register
source → authority type → edition/status → scope → applicability → requirement/claim → evidence → owner → supplier flow-down → retention → change trigger.

## Listener tool — Standards Change Impact Check
what changed? which active requirement references it? which evidence/procedure/supplier flow-down depends on it? what must be reassessed?

## Misconceptions
- standard = law;
- certification = product compliance;
- newest edition always automatically applies to every existing contract;
- guidance is mandatory;
- customer requirement is optional;
- a PDF library is an applicability system.

## Closing action
Take one standard your team says it “follows” and write one explicit applicability statement and one evidence link.
