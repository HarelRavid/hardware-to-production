## Listener promise
By the end, the listener can tell the difference between “we have supplier names” and “we have controlled, qualified production capability that can survive change and disruption.”

## Cold open
[ILLUSTRATIVE]
Primary supplier performs well for a year, then changes a sub-tier process. Defects rise. The backup supplier is on the approved-vendor list but has never built the current revision.

Question: Do you have two suppliers—or one supplier plus a spreadsheet?

## Beat 1 — Approval is an evidence envelope
Tags: [SYNTHESIS]
Claims: EP40-C03
Wave 01 + Wave 02 method context.
Teach: product revision, process/site/tooling/material/sub-tier/test/rate can define the demonstrated envelope.

## Beat 2 — Supplier performance dimensions
Tags: [AUTH-GUIDANCE] [SYNTHESIS]
Claims: EP40-C01/C02/C07
Source: W2-S11
NIST MEP publicly supports supplier evaluation/selection, TCO, segmentation and metrics/scorecards.
Guard: no universal weighting formula.

## Beat 3 — Scorecards cannot erase hard stops
Tags: [SYNTHESIS]
Claims: EP40-C08
Example: great delivery score cannot offset unresolved safety-critical escape/change violation.
Use separate dimensions and escalation gates.

## Beat 4 — Incoming inspection
Tags: [SYNTHESIS]
Claims: EP40-C06
Teach: incoming inspection is one control and can be valuable. It is not automatically equivalent to controlling a source-process mechanism that creates latent/critical variation.

## Beat 5 — Supplier/process change
Tags: [SYNTHESIS] [CUSTOMER/SECTOR GATED]
Claims: EP40-C04
Sequence:
change → impact → notification per applicable agreement → evidence → effectivity → monitoring.
Guard: no universal mandatory notification trigger list.

## Beat 6 — Dual sourcing
Tags: [SYNTHESIS]
Claims: EP40-C05
Teach: second source can use a different process route and still be valid if required claims are independently demonstrated.
Check: current revision, material/process, tooling, measurement, test, deviations, capacity, genealogy, lead time.

## Beat 7 — Common-mode risk
Tags: [SYNTHESIS]
Example: two suppliers depend on the same sub-tier resin/tooling/software/special process.
Resilience requires dependency map, not logo count.

## Beat 8 — Supplier Resilience Map
Tool:
part/capability → current source → process/sub-tier → quality/capability evidence → change rules → capacity/logistics → alternate → evidence gaps → recovery trigger.
Run Sentinel connector source example.

## DEV / LVP / SVP
DEV: learn supplier/process options; avoid false permanent approval.
LVP: qualification/effectivity/change ownership becomes explicit.
SVP: monitor sustained performance, sub-tiers, capacity, alternates and recovery.

## Misconceptions
- approved vendor = approved forever.
- two suppliers = resilience.
- incoming inspection fixes supplier process weakness.
- one weighted supplier score captures risk.
- alternate source must use identical process.
- resilience is only geography.

## Closing action
Pick one critical purchased part and answer: if the current supplier stopped tomorrow, what exactly must be transferred, rebuilt or re-proven before another source can ship acceptable product?

## Season handoff
Season 4 ends with the external production system under evidence control. Season 5 asks what happens when we automate and connect that system without scaling its instability.
