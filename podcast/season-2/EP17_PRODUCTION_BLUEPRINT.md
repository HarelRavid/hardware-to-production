# Episode 17 Production Blueprint — Additive Manufacturing: When It Creates Value and When It Does Not

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 2 — How Hardware Is Actually Made
lifecycle: DEV → LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: sees AM mainly as a fast/progressive way to make complex parts.
After: can decide where AM creates genuine product/economic value, what evidence it needs as production, and when another route is superior.

## Narrative hook
A topology-optimized metal part looks perfect for AM. Then supports, build orientation, post-machining, inspection access and qualification burden erase the apparent advantage. The geometry was printable, but the process chain was not competitive.

## Teaching flow
1. AM family map: polymer extrusion/resin/powder concepts and metal PBF/DED/binder-style routes at a decision level.
2. Where AM creates value: complexity, internal channels, consolidation, customization, low tooling, iteration and low-volume economics.
3. Orientation/supports and anisotropy/process-direction effects.
4. Surface/dimensional limits and post-processing.
5. Material feedstock/build history and traceability.
6. Defects, porosity, residual stress/distortion and process monitoring concepts.
7. Inspection/CT and accessibility burden.
8. Production qualification vs prototype printing.
9. The explicit decision: when should we NOT use AM?

## Core framework — AM Value Test
`Unique AM value → part/process requirements → build/orientation → supports → material/process risks → post-processing → inspection/qualification → rate/cost → alternative route comparison → decision`.

## Worked example
A fluid manifold combines several machined parts and internal channels. AM may reduce assembly/joints and enable internal geometry, but only if pressure/surface/tolerance/material evidence and post-process inspection can be controlled economically. A simple bracket with no AM-specific value likely remains better suited to machining/forming/casting.

## Listener tools
1. AM Value Test.
2. Production-AM Evidence Checklist: machine/build config, feedstock lot/state, orientation, supports, heat treatment/HIP if used, machining, inspection and final genealogy.

## Standards/source architecture
Priority families include ISO/ASTM 52900-series and process/material/application-specific requirements. Current editions/status and exact scope must be verified before normative statements. Material allowables, parameter windows, qualification and acceptance limits are not transferable across AM processes/machines/materials without evidence.

## Common mistakes
- printable = production viable;
- AM complexity is free;
- prototype print proves qualified production;
- ignoring support removal/post-machining/inspection;
- comparing printer time to final good-part cost;
- using AM where no AM-specific value exists.
