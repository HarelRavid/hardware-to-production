# Episode 7 Production Blueprint — Design for Assembly and Mistake Prevention

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 2 — Turn the Prototype into a Product

## Listener transformation
The listener learns to see assembly quality as a design/process interaction and to reduce dependence on tacit knowledge, memory and downstream inspection.

## Narrative hook
The inventor can assemble the product flawlessly because they know which cable to route first, how far the connector must “feel” seated and which fastener must be approached at an angle. The first technician builds the same design and creates intermittent failures. Nothing changed in the drawing — only the hidden knowledge disappeared.

## Teaching flow
1. Why expert prototype assembly hides risk.
2. DFA beyond part-count reduction.
3. Orientation, access, sequence, identification, keying, datums/fixtures and joining inputs.
4. Distinguish design-induced ambiguity from “operator error.”
5. Mistake-Proofing Ladder: eliminate, prevent, guide/constrain, detect at source, contain, verify downstream, instruction-only.
6. Connector and fastener worked examples.
7. Assembly Risk Walk exercise.
8. Control effectiveness, bypass and change/maintenance impact.

## Listener tool — Assembly Risk Walk
For each operation capture: `Operation → Orientation → Access → Sequence → Identification → Controlled force/torque/energy → Fixture/datum → Hidden defect → Verification → Rework/recovery → Human dependency → Design opportunity`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: identify where expert knowledge rescues ambiguity. LVP: record defects/rework/operator variation and introduce targeted fixtures/design controls. SVP: demonstrate assembly controls, tools, verification, traceability and recovery at intended rate.

## Common mistakes
- “Fewer parts is always better.”
- Blaming operator error without examining architecture/tooling/work method.
- Adding inspection instead of removing an easy hidden error.
- Assuming poka-yoke eliminates validation and training.
- Treating final torque value alone as universal joint proof.

## Source/evidence backlog
Formal poka-yoke terminology, human-factors claims, torque/joint rules and sector-specific assembly controls remain source/applicability gated.

## Closing handoff
Episode 7 makes correct assembly easier. Episode 8 asks how the product itself enables programming, calibration, test and traceable release evidence.
