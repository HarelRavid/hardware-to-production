# Episode 4 Production Blueprint — Product Readiness vs Manufacturing Readiness

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 2 — Turn the Prototype into a Product

## Listener transformation
The listener learns to separate product evidence from manufacturing evidence and to make readiness decisions without compressing everything into one score.

## Narrative hook
A product passes its functional and environmental tests, yet production still cannot build it reliably. In another case, the line is stable but the design changes underneath it. Both situations feel “almost ready,” but for completely different reasons.

## Teaching flow
1. Define product-readiness evidence versus manufacturing-readiness evidence.
2. Show why they are coupled but not identical.
3. Product dimensions: requirements, architecture/interfaces, configuration, performance, reliability/environment, safety/compliance.
4. Manufacturing dimensions: process, supplier, tooling, work method, measurement/test, traceability, yield/rework, capacity, economics, change/recovery.
5. Introduce Hardware Manufacturing Readiness Matrix.
6. Sentinel example: product verified enough for LVP while calibration station and connector process remain immature.
7. Explain readiness relative to the next commitment, not an abstract final state.

## Listener tool — Hardware Manufacturing Readiness Matrix
For every dimension record: `Claim → Evidence → Gap → Owner → Next commitment → Closure evidence`. Do not average into one percentage.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: enough evidence to justify the next learning investment. LVP: bounded configuration plus controlled population learning. SVP: product and manufacturing evidence coexist within the intended commercial envelope.

## Common mistakes
- One red/yellow/green dashboard hiding different risk types.
- Treating successful verification as process capability.
- Treating stable process output as proof of product compliance.
- Demanding every dimension mature at the same rate.

## Source/evidence backlog
Any named external readiness model or maturity level requires exact source and applicability. The matrix remains internal synthesis.

## Closing handoff
Episode 4 defines readiness dimensions; Episode 5 shows how to use lifecycle gates without letting EVT/DVT/PVT labels replace those dimensions.
