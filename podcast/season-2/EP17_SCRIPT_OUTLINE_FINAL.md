## Listener transformation
Before:
“AM is the advanced way to make complex parts.”

After:
“I can tell when AM creates genuine product/economic value, what evidence burden it creates, and when a conventional or hybrid route is better.”

## Two-character opportunity
Rona:
“If the part is complex and I can print it without tooling, why wouldn’t I?”

Oz:
Because printable geometry may create support, post-processing, inspection, qualification and cost burdens that erase the advantage.

## Cold open
[ILLUSTRATIVE]
A topology-optimized metal part prints beautifully, but supports, machining, inaccessible internal surfaces and inspection/qualification turn it into a poor production route.

## Beat 1 — AM family map, briefly
Use ISO/ASTM 52900 vocabulary only at high level.
Do not catalogue every technology.

## Beat 2 — AM Value Test
Claim: EP17-C02.
Ask:
- What unique value exists?
- Internal geometry?
- Consolidation?
- Customization?
- Tooling avoidance?
- Iteration speed?
- Supply-chain advantage?

If there is no AM-specific value, compare conventional routes seriously.

## Beat 3 — Orientation/supports/build strategy
Claim: EP17-C03.
Show same geometry in two build orientations.
Discuss support, surface, distortion, post-machining and evidence.

## Beat 4 — Material/process state
Claim: EP17-C04.
Anisotropy / residual stress / porosity / surface state.
No universal values.

## Beat 5 — Post-processing is part of the route
Claim: EP17-C05.
Heat treatment/HIP/machining/polishing/cleaning where applicable.
Do not treat printed geometry as final part state automatically.

## Beat 6 — Internal geometry creates verification debt
Claim: EP17-C06.
Cleaning / CT/NDE / dimensional access / trapped material / surface verification.

## Beat 7 — Qualification layers
Claim: EP17-C07.
Machine/system vs process vs part/application qualification.
Use ISO/ASTM 52920 and TS 52930 as scoped context.

## Beat 8 — Prototype print vs production
Claim: EP17-C08.
A successful prototype is evidence for a narrower claim.

## Beat 9 — In-process monitoring
Claim: EP17-C12.
Monitoring can help, but does not replace final evidence unless correlation is validated.

## Beat 10 — Economics
Claims: EP17-C10/C11.
Compare:
build
+ setup
+ supports
+ post-process
+ inspection
+ scrap/rework
+ qualification
+ accepted final output.

No break-even volume folklore.

## Misconceptions
- printable = production viable;
- complexity is free;
- prototype print = qualified production;
- printer time = final part cost;
- in-process monitoring removes need for qualification;
- internal channels are automatically a benefit.

## Closing action
Take one AM candidate and write the unique value that justifies AM. If you cannot, force a conventional-route comparison.
