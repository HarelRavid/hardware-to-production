## 1. Episode promise
Define NPI/industrialization as coordinated closure of cross-functional evidence and execution gaps between product development and repeatable production — not merely a late handoff, project tracker or fixture-building function.

Canonical listener question:
> What must NPI actually own or coordinate before a working prototype can become a controlled production system?

## 3. Core thesis
NPI is not inherently a department that receives a finished design. It is the coordinated closure of the product/process/supplier/test/quality/configuration/economics evidence and execution system required for repeatable production.

The exact org structure varies. This episode defines responsibilities, not universal job titles.

## 4. Audience contract
### DEV takeaway
Bring manufacturing, test and supplier constraints into development while changes are still economically reversible, without prematurely freezing the design.

### Prototype shortcut
NPI responsibilities can be distributed across existing people while volume/risk is low if ownership remains explicit.

### Shortcut expiration
Once industrialization decisions span teams/suppliers and create irreversible cost or release evidence, cross-functional ownership cannot remain informal.

### LVP change
NPI becomes a coordination layer that turns product definition into controlled build/test/supplier/process evidence across multiple units.

### SVP evidence
Industrialization must demonstrate that product, process, supplier, measurement, configuration, rate and release controls operate coherently at the intended commercial scale/envelope.

### Manufacturing-debt prevention
Do not wait for “design complete” to discover the product cannot be assembled, tested, sourced, traced or recovered from defects economically.

### Listener action
Map every major industrialization workstream to an owner and identify the five outputs most exposed by unclear ownership.

## 6. NPI responsibility domains
NPI/industrialization commonly ensures ownership or coordination across:
- production-intent configuration;
- process route and work sequence;
- tooling/fixtures/equipment;
- supplier readiness and changes;
- CTQs/inspection/test/calibration;
- work instructions/operator method;
- traceability/genealogy;
- pilot build learning;
- yield/rework/NCR feedback;
- capacity/rate;
- manufacturing economics;
- release/handback/change/effectivity.

This is a responsibility map, not a mandatory department structure.

## 8. Industrialization Handoff Risk Review
Ask:
1. Which prototype steps still require inventor knowledge?
2. Which product characteristics have no production CTQ/test/control owner?
3. Which suppliers/components remain prototype-only or substitution-prone?
4. Which process/tooling decisions are being made before evidence is ready?
5. Can every pilot unit be reconstructed by configuration and deviation history?
6. What happens when a build fails — who owns containment, diagnosis and design/process feedback?
7. Is rate/capacity modeled from accepted good output or only nominal cycle time?
8. Which changes can enter production without defined effectivity ownership?
9. What evidence is required before the next LVP/SVP commitment?

## 9. Sentinel Node example
Engineering has a working integrated prototype. Industrialization exposes gaps: connector insertion needs controlled method/fixture; programming/calibration needs repeatable station/procedure; enclosure source/process remains bridge intent; CTQs and inspection need definition; PCB/BOM/FW configurations must link to serial identity; pilot builds need yield/rework learning objectives; calibration may constrain rate; supplier/recipe changes need controlled effectivity.

NPI need not own every technical decision. It must ensure these dependencies become one executable industrialization system with explicit owners, evidence and decision boundaries.

## 11. Common failure modes
- NPI invited after tooling purchase.
- NPI reduced to project tracking without technical evidence ownership.
- Manufacturing engineering inherits unresolved product ambiguity.
- Quality is asked to create controls after critical design/process choices are frozen.
- Supplier readiness is separated from design/process change decisions.
- Pilot build produces units but no explicit learning/evidence closure.
- Documents are complete but no one owns the exit decision.

## 12. Applicability statement
This episode teaches a general NPI/industrialization responsibility model. Companies and industries distribute these responsibilities differently among systems, product, NPI, manufacturing engineering, operations, quality, supply chain and program management. No universal org chart or job title is claimed.

## 13. What this episode must NOT claim
- every startup needs a dedicated NPI department immediately;
- NPI owns product design decisions unilaterally;
- industrialization begins only after design freeze;
- document completion proves readiness;
- one pilot build proves process capability;
- manufacturing engineering and NPI are universally identical roles.
