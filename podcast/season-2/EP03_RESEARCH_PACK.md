## 3. Core thesis
NPI is not a department that receives a finished design.

It is the coordinated closure of the evidence and execution system needed to produce the intended product repeatedly.

The exact org structure varies, but NPI/industrialization commonly must ensure ownership or coordination across:
- production-intent configuration;
- process route and work sequence;
- tooling/fixtures/equipment;
- supplier readiness and changes;
- CTQs/inspection/test/calibration;
- work instructions/operator method;
- traceability/genealogy;
- pilot build learning;
- yield/rework/NCR feedback;
- capacity/rate;
- manufacturing economics;
- release/handback/change control.

This is a responsibility map, not a universal job description.

## 4. Audience contract
### DEV takeaway
Bring manufacturing/test/supplier constraints into development while changes are still cheap, without prematurely freezing the design.

### Prototype shortcut
NPI responsibilities can be distributed across existing people while volume/risk is low if ownership remains explicit.

### Shortcut expiration
Once industrialization decisions span teams/suppliers and create irreversible cost or release evidence, cross-functional ownership cannot remain informal.

### LVP change
NPI becomes the coordination layer that turns product definition into controlled build/test/supplier/process evidence across multiple units.

### SVP evidence
Industrialization must demonstrate that product, process, supplier, measurement, configuration, rate and release controls operate coherently at commercial scale.

### Manufacturing-debt prevention
Do not wait for “design complete” to discover that the product cannot be assembled, tested, sourced, traced or recovered from defects economically.

### Listener action
Map every major industrialization workstream to an owner and identify which five outputs are currently blocked by unclear ownership.

## 7. Industrialization Handoff Risk Review
Ask:
1. Which prototype steps still require inventor knowledge?
2. Which product characteristics have no production CTQ/test/control owner?
3. Which suppliers/components are still prototype-only or substitution-prone?
4. Which process/tooling decisions are being made before evidence is ready?
5. Can every pilot unit be reconstructed by configuration and deviation history?
6. What happens when a build fails — who owns containment, diagnosis and design/process feedback?
7. Is rate/capacity modeled from good accepted output or nominal cycle time?
8. Which change decisions can currently enter production without a defined effectivity owner?
9. What evidence is required before the next LVP/SVP commitment?

## 8. Sentinel Node example
Engineering has a working integrated prototype.

NPI workstreams expose gaps:
- connector insertion needs a controlled fixture/method;
- programming/calibration needs repeatable station/procedure;
- enclosure supplier/material/process remains bridge intent;
- CTQs and inspection logic need definition;
- PCB/BOM/FW configurations must be linked to serial identity;
- pilot build needs yield/rework learning objectives;
- calibration may be the rate constraint;
- supplier/recipe changes need controlled effectivity.

The NPI job is not to own every technical decision. It is to make sure these cross-functional dependencies become one executable industrialization system with explicit owners and evidence.

## 9. Common failure modes
- NPI invited after tooling purchase.
- NPI reduced to project tracking without technical evidence ownership.
- Manufacturing engineer becomes owner of product ambiguity instead of escalating it.
- Quality is asked to “make a control plan” after process/design decisions are already frozen.
- Supplier quality/readiness is separated from design/process change decisions.
- Pilot build produces units but no explicit learning/evidence closure.
- NPI creates documents but nobody owns the exit decision.

## 12. What this episode must NOT claim

- NPI owns product design decisions unilaterally;
- industrialization begins only after design freeze;
- document completion proves readiness;
- one pilot build proves process capability;
- manufacturing engineering and NPI are universally identical roles.
