# Season 2 Full Review — How Hardware Is Actually Made

status: PASS WITH CONTROLLED EVIDENCE BACKLOG
scope: Episodes 11–19 production blueprints

## 1. Season mission result
PASS.

Season 2 successfully transforms the listener from “I know what the part/product needs to do” into a manufacturing-route decision posture: process choice is justified through function, material/state, geometry, tolerance/surface, production stage, volume, tooling/NRE, supplier capability, inspection, yield, secondary operations and cost per accepted good unit.

Canonical learning arc:
`Process selection → Polymers → Metals → Joining → Composites → Ceramics/Powders/Sintering → Additive → Surface/Cleaning/Heat Treatment → Process Chains`.

## 2. Learning progression audit
PASS.

EP11 establishes the reusable route-selection logic. EP12–EP18 apply that logic to distinct manufacturing families without pretending one family is universally more mature. EP19 then closes the season by showing that the final engineering object is often a chain of state transformations, not one isolated process.

The progression deliberately moves from choosing a primary route to understanding process-created material state, joining and secondary operations, and finally to cumulative variation/yield/evidence across the chain.

## 7. Audience and DEV/LVP/SVP audit
PASS.

Audience A learns to avoid prototype-route lock-in and premature tooling/process commitments.
Audience B receives practical route, supplier, evidence and process-chain tools for LVP/industrialization.

Each episode states how bridge manufacturing may be legitimate in DEV/LVP and what evidence/economic trigger forces reassessment before SVP.

## 8. Visual/teaching audit
PASS WITH SCRIPTING REQUIREMENT.

This season is unusually visual. Production scripts should be supported by show-note diagrams or companion graphics for process physics that audio alone cannot carry efficiently: mold flow/warpage, grain flow, weld/adhesive joint mechanisms, laminate architecture, sintering shrinkage, AM orientation/supports, surface/coating interactions and end-to-end process chains.

The audio should teach the decision; the visual should carry spatial/process detail.

## 9. Editorial risks to manage during scripting
1. Do not let EP11 become a generic matrix lecture; anchor it in competing real routes.
2. EP12–EP18 must avoid encyclopedic catalogues; teach mechanism → trade-off → decision.
3. Keep exact numeric heuristics behind verified source/applicability gates.
4. Avoid implying “serial production” always means dedicated hard tooling.
5. Use the same Sentinel/component examples across several episodes where useful, but change the decision question rather than repeating the story.
6. EP16 must separate ceramics/powder-metal general principles from product-specific sintering recipes.
7. EP17 must actively challenge AM hype rather than acting as a process showcase.
8. EP19 must remain integrative and not repeat every prior process family.

## 10. Season handoff
PASS.

Season 2 ends with the listener able to justify not only how an individual feature/part should be made but how operations must be sequenced into a controlled route with valid evidence.

Season 3 can therefore begin with:
> We know how the part should be made. How do we build the production system that executes the route repeatedly across people, tools, suppliers, units and changes?

Canonical Season 3 scope: Episodes 20–31.
