# Episode 15 Production Blueprint — Composite Manufacturing

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 2 — How Hardware Is Actually Made
lifecycle: DEV → LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: treats “carbon fiber” or “composite” as a material label.
After: understands that fiber architecture, resin, layup, cure and defects are inseparable from final part behavior and production evidence.

## Narrative hook
A hand-laid carbon prototype is light and stiff. A later supplier part uses the same nominal fiber/resin family but behaves differently because orientation, cure, void content and local details changed. The material name did not define the product.

## Teaching flow
1. Composite system = reinforcement + matrix + architecture + process.
2. Main routes: hand layup, prepreg/vacuum/autoclave concepts, infusion/RTM, compression molding and automated layup where relevant.
3. Fiber orientation, ply drops, corners and load paths.
4. Resin/cure, temperature/time/pressure and storage/handling.
5. Consolidation, voids, porosity, wrinkles, delamination and contamination.
6. Tooling, trim/drill, inserts and bonded joints.
7. Coupon evidence vs representative article evidence.
8. Inspection/NDT and process traceability.
9. DEV/LVP craft knowledge vs SVP controlled recipes/material lots/equipment.

## Core framework — Composite Evidence Chain
`Function/load → laminate/material architecture → forming/layup route → cure/consolidation → defect mechanisms → trim/join → inspection → mechanical evidence → configuration/lot/process traceability`.

## Worked example
A lightweight cover is first hand-laid for geometry and stiffness learning. Before production transfer, the team must decide whether fiber orientation, resin/cure, thickness distribution, bonded inserts and environmental evidence are representative enough for the intended claims.

## Listener tools
1. Composite Process Definition Card.
2. Coupon-to-Part Transfer Check: geometry, layup, thickness, cure, defects, environment, loading and inspection equivalence.

## Visual asset plan
- Ply orientation/load-path diagram.
- Vacuum-bag stack sketch.
- Common defect illustrations: wrinkle, void, delamination.
- Coupon vs complex-part evidence comparison.

## Standards/source architecture
Map ASTM/ISO composite test methods, material specifications, aerospace/customer process requirements and NDT methods as applicable. No allowable property, cure condition, void limit or acceptance threshold is universal; exact material/process specification governs.

## Common mistakes
- material trade name = complete material definition;
- coupon performance = automatic part performance;
- ignoring storage/out-time/cure history;
- treating trimming/drilling/bonding as minor secondary steps;
- relying on artisan skill without visible process controls;
- changing supplier/material form without evidence-impact review.
