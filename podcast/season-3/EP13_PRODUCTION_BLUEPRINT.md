# Episode 13 Production Blueprint — Metal Parts: Casting, Forming or Machining?

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 3 — Manufacturing Processes
lifecycle: DEV → LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: treats machining as the default metal route.
After: can compare casting, forging/forming, extrusion, sheet processes, machining and near-net + finish machining based on product and production evidence.

## Narrative hook
A machined aluminum prototype survives testing. At higher volume, a cast or formed route is proposed. Suddenly grain flow, porosity, heat treatment, residual stress, distortion and stock allowance become part of the engineering problem.

## Teaching flow
1. Metal process-family map.
2. Machining/material removal: strengths, access, datum and stock implications.
3. Casting families: near-net geometry, defects, feeding/solidification concepts and finish machining.
4. Forging/forming/extrusion: flow, anisotropy, section limits and tooling.
5. Sheet-metal logic: bend/form sequence, springback, edge/feature implications.
6. Material state: alloy, temper, heat treatment and residual stress.
7. Near-net + machining process chains.
8. Inspection/NDT and CTQ implications.
9. DEV/LVP/SVP economics and supplier capability.

## Core framework — Metal Route Matrix
`Load/function → alloy/state → geometry/section → route family → defect/variation modes → heat treatment/state changes → machining/finish allowance → inspection/NDT → volume/tooling → supplier evidence`.

## Worked example
A structural bracket begins as 5-axis CNC billet in DEV. LVP remains machining because design is moving. SVP evaluation compares forging + finish machining against casting + machining and continued CNC using load path, material properties, dimensional controls, tooling/NRE, scrap, supplier maturity and inspection burden.

## Listener tools

2. Material-State Check: incoming state, transformation, thermal history, final property evidence, distortion risk and inspection.

## Standards/source architecture
Target source families include ASTM/ISO/SAE/AMS material specifications, heat-treatment standards, dimensional/NDT standards and supplier process qualifications as applicable. No generic strength, porosity, machining allowance or heat-treatment requirement will be treated as universal.

## Common mistakes
- choosing process solely by geometry;
- assuming same alloy name means same final material state;
- ignoring distortion after thermal processing;
- machining critical datums before a later operation moves them;
- comparing unit prices without scrap, inspection and secondary ops;
- assuming theoretical process capability equals supplier capability.
