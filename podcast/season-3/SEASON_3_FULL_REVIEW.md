## 5. DEV/LVP/SVP continuity
PASS.

Every process family preserves the same rule: DEV optimizes learning, LVP may use deliberate bridge routes, and SVP requires evidence that the chosen route supports the commercial quality/rate/supply/economic envelope. No fixed quantity is used as a universal trigger for process transition.

## 6. Standards/source burden
Season 3 is the most standards-heavy season so far and remains intentionally source-gated.

Highest-priority verification areas:
- Ep12: polymer material/test standards plus material/supplier processing guidance;
- Ep13: ASTM/ISO/SAE/AMS material state, heat treatment and NDT where used;
- Ep14: AWS/ASME/ISO joining qualifications, IPC/J-STD for electronics soldering where applicable, adhesive/fastener standards;
- Ep15: ASTM/ISO composite test methods, material/process specifications and NDT;
- Ep16: ceramic/powder metallurgy standards, density/material characterization and thermal-process evidence;
- Ep17: ISO/ASTM 52900-series exact editions/scope and application qualification;
- Ep18: coating/cleanliness/corrosion sources plus applicable AMS heat-treatment/pyrometry standards;
- Ep19: sector/customer routing, special-process, traceability and requalification requirements.

No numerical design limit, process window, acceptance threshold or qualification requirement is considered publication-ready until exact source/applicability is verified.

## 10. Editorial risks to control during scripting
1. Avoid process encyclopedias; keep every section tied to a decision/failure.
2. Do not universalize supplier handbook heuristics into standards.
3. Distinguish material specification from manufacturing capability.
4. Use real defect/failure images only with provenance/licensing control.
5. Do not imply that one special process is universally superior.
6. Keep process-specific acronyms/jargon below the listener's prerequisite burden.
7. Explicitly label illustrative process limits/economics when not field data.
