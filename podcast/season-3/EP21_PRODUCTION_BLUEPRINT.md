# Episode 21 Production Blueprint — Tooling, Jigs, Fixtures and Production-Intent Equipment

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 3 — Build the Factory Before You Need the Factory
lifecycle: LVP → Production Validation → Ramp

## Listener transformation
Before: sees fixtures as shop aids created after design.
After: can define tooling by the product/process claim it must control, including locating, clamping, mistake prevention, wear, maintenance, calibration and changeability.

## Narrative hook
A printed jig makes ten units successfully. By unit sixty its locating surface wears, operators compensate by feel, and measurement drift appears. The fixture was useful — but nobody defined when it stopped being valid evidence.

## Teaching flow
1. Tooling categories: locating, clamping, joining, test, handling and inspection.
2. Datum transfer from product definition into fixture design.
3. Repeatability versus operator compensation.
4. Poka-yoke and wrong-part/wrong-orientation prevention.
5. Wear, maintenance, calibration/verification and spare strategy.
6. Changeover and product-revision compatibility.
7. Bridge tooling versus production-intent tooling.
8. Equipment URS/acceptance logic at the right maturity.

## Core framework — Tooling Evidence Card
`Operation/claim → product datum/interface → locate/clamp/control method → error prevented → verification method → wear/maintenance → calibration/health check → revision/effectivity → expiration trigger`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: improvised tooling is acceptable when limitations are visible. LVP: fixtures should remove recurring operator rescue and preserve evidence. SVP: equipment/tooling capability, maintenance, spares, change control and acceptance must support intended rate.

## Common mistakes
- fixture success on a few units treated as long-term capability;
- locating from cosmetic/nonfunctional surfaces;
- over-constraining flexible parts;
- no wear/health check;
- fixture changes not linked to product/process revision;
- automating unstable manual logic.

## Source/evidence backlog
Sector-specific equipment qualification, calibration and machinery-safety requirements remain applicability gated. Numerical fixture tolerances require product/process evidence.

## Closing handoff
Episode 21 creates repeatable physical execution. Episode 22 turns expert operator knowledge into controlled standard work.
