# Episode 23 Production Blueprint — DFMEA, PFMEA, Control Plans and Quality Gates

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 3 — Build the Factory Before You Need the Factory
lifecycle: LVP → Production Validation → Ramp

## Listener transformation
Before: sees FMEA/control plans as quality documents.
After: uses them as a connected engineering chain from failure risk to prevention, detection, reaction and effectiveness evidence.

## Narrative hook
A defect is found at final inspection. The team adds another check. Six weeks later the same failure returns because the actual process mechanism was never changed. Detection improved; control did not.

## Teaching flow
1. Product/design risk versus process risk.
2. Failure mode, mechanism/cause, effect and control.
3. Prevention versus detection.
4. CTQs/process characteristics and measurement link.
5. Control plan as execution layer, not duplicate PFMEA.
6. Quality gates and explicit reaction plans.
7. Effectiveness feedback from NCR/rework/yield.
8. Automotive AIAG/VDA terminology as scoped methodology, not universal law.

## Core framework — Risk-to-Control Chain
`Requirement/function → failure risk → process step → mechanism/cause → characteristic → prevention → detection/measurement → gate → reaction → effectiveness evidence`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: lightweight risk register and targeted controls. LVP: PFMEA/control logic should follow real build learning. SVP: controls, reaction plans, measurement and change discipline must remain linked to demonstrated risks.

## Common mistakes
- RPN/AP score treated as the engineering conclusion;
- detection added instead of prevention;
- PFMEA and control plan drifting apart;
- no reaction plan after out-of-control/failed result;
- quality gates with no authority or containment definition.

## Source/evidence backlog
AIAG & VDA FMEA Handbook, AIAG Control Plan/APQP and sector-specific requirements remain edition/applicability gated. Do not publish proprietary tables or universalize automotive practice.

## Closing handoff
Episode 23 defines what must be controlled. Episode 24 asks whether production test and measurement can actually distinguish good from bad reliably.
