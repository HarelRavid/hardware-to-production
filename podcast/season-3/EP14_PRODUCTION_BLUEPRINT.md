# Episode 14 Production Blueprint — Joining: Welding, Adhesives, Brazing, Soldering and Fasteners

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 3 — Manufacturing Processes
lifecycle: DEV → LVP → SVP

## Listener transformation
Before: chooses a joining method by familiarity or “strength.”
After: selects a joining route from load path, materials, permanence/serviceability, sealing/electrical/thermal needs, process variation, inspection, repair and qualification evidence.

## Narrative hook
Two joints both pass a bench pull test. One later loosens under cycling; the other corrodes at an interface. The mistake was not simply choosing the “wrong” joining process — it was defining the joint by static strength instead of its real service and production requirements.

## Teaching flow
1. Start with joint function and failure consequence.
2. Mechanical fasteners: preload/locking/access/serviceability.
3. Welding families: heat input, distortion, metallurgy, fixture/operator/process effects.
4. Brazing/soldering: filler, wetting, thermal cycle, cleanliness and joint geometry.
5. Adhesives: surface prep, bond-line, cure, environment and process control.
6. Hybrid joints and why adding methods can add failure modes.
7. Inspectability and destructive/non-destructive evidence.
8. Repair/rework and effect on product configuration.
9. Manual LVP vs qualified/monitored/automated SVP process.

## Core framework — Joining Requirement Sheet
`Joint function → load/environment → materials/coatings → permanence/serviceability → candidate process → preparation → fixture/access → critical process variables → inspection/test → repair → qualification/effectivity`.

## Worked example
Sealed electronics enclosure: compare screws + gasket, structural adhesive, weld/braze options where material permits. Evaluate sealing, service, thermal distortion, contamination, cycle time, inspection, field repair and change risk rather than one “strength” number.

## Listener tools
1. Joining Process Selection Logic.
2. Joint Evidence Checklist: coupon/process qualification vs actual geometry, operator/equipment state, inspection and service environment.

## Visual asset plan

## Standards/source architecture
Exact source families depend on joining type and industry: AWS/ASME/ISO welding/brazing qualifications, IPC/J-STD for electronics soldering where applicable, ASTM/ISO adhesive tests, fastener/torque/material standards and customer requirements. Qualification, acceptance and operator-certification claims remain blocked until exact applicable editions are verified.

## Common mistakes
- “strongest joint” thinking;
- ignoring load direction/fatigue/environment;
- using coupon strength as automatic assembly proof;
- treating surface preparation as housekeeping;
- specifying torque without joint/friction context;
- designing an uninspectable/unrepairable critical joint by accident.
