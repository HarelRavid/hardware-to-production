# Episode 30 Production Blueprint — Supplier Readiness and Contract-Manufacturer Management

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 3 — Build the Factory Before You Need the Factory
lifecycle: LVP → Production Validation → Ramp

## Listener transformation
Before: judges supplier readiness mainly by sample quality and quoted capacity.
After: can evaluate source readiness through configuration, process, capability, capacity, quality evidence, change control, traceability and technical-interface discipline.

## Narrative hook
A supplier delivers ten perfect prototype parts. Ramp begins and defects appear because production moves to a different machine, sub-tier source and inspection method. The sample was good; the production source was never validated as a system.

## Teaching flow
1. Prototype supplier versus production-intent source.
2. Technical package and configuration clarity.
3. Process/equipment/site/sub-tier identity.
4. Supplier capability and measurement evidence.
5. Capacity/lead-time and recovery evidence.
6. Incoming inspection versus source control.
7. Change notification/effectivity and substitutions.
8. Contract manufacturer boundaries: who owns process, tooling, test, quality data and engineering change?
9. Escalation and supplier-development loops during ramp.

## Core framework — Supplier Readiness Card
`Part/process claim → source/site/process identity → technical package → capability evidence → measurement/inspection → capacity/lead time → quality history → change control → traceability/sub-tier → recovery plan → release status`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: fast suppliers and flexible substitutions may be rational if evidence limits are explicit. LVP: production-intent sources should become identifiable and controlled. Ramp/SVP: source capability, capacity, change discipline and resilience must support the commercial envelope.

## Common mistakes
- sample approval treated as source approval;
- theoretical machine capacity treated as supplier capacity;
- incoming inspection used to compensate indefinitely for unstable source process;
- unauthorized sub-tier/material/process substitutions;
- CM ownership gaps around test, rework, tooling and change;
- commercial terms disconnected from technical change control.

## Source/evidence backlog
ISO 9001/IATF/AS9100/PPAP/customer supplier requirements are applicability dependent. Do not universalize automotive/aerospace supplier-approval practices.

## Closing handoff
Episode 30 aligns external production with the ramp. Episode 31 closes the season by controlling engineering change while material, WIP, suppliers and released units are already moving.
