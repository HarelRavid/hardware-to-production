## Listener promise
Expose hidden factory rescue by separating first-pass success, rework/repair, scrap and eventual good output—and use recurrence as the learning signal.

## Cold open
[ILLUSTRATIVE] Final yield says 96%, but one in three units was opened, adjusted and retested before passing.

## Beat 1 — Define the episode's yield language
Claims: EP28-C01
Guard: organizations use yield terms differently; state our terms explicitly.
First-pass good vs eventual accepted output.

## Beat 2 — Rework history is production evidence
Claims: EP28-C02
Dependency: Wave 01/02.
A final PASS is a state, not the whole history.

## Beat 3 — Rework consumes time and constraint capacity
Claims: EP28-C03
Sources: W3-S07/S08 + arithmetic model.
Explain why recovered units are not free.

## Beat 4 — Defect Pareto and population context
Claims: EP28-C05
Segment by configuration/process/supplier/operator/equipment when relevant.

## Beat 5 — Containment vs corrective learning
Claims: EP28-C04
Contain now; then test whether corrective action changed future populations.

## Beat 6 — Learning curve without mythology
Claims: EP28-C07
Ask what actually changed: design, fixture, WI, skill, source, measurement, maintenance.
No generic learning-rate percentage.

## Beat 7 — Yield Learning Ledger
Use connector issue: initial fail → rework → retest → time/cost → cause/action → recurrence.

## Beat 8 — Capacity/economics bridge
Rework at a bottleneck reduces accepted throughput and changes cost per good unit. Preview EP29/P2.05.

## Misconceptions
- final yield is enough;
- rework is free if unit passes;
- scrap reduction automatically means process improvement;
- all yield should be blended together;
- learning curve happens automatically with volume.
