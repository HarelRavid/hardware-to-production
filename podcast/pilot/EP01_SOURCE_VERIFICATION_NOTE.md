## Purpose

Provide authoritative support for the central Episode 1 distinction without importing defense-acquisition maturity models as universal startup requirements.

## Verified source anchors

### GAO — production-representative evidence and manufacturing readiness

Primary sources:
- GAO-10-439, *Best Practices: DOD Can Achieve Better Outcomes by Standardizing the Way Manufacturing Risks Are Managed*.
- GAO-06-368, *Defense Acquisitions: Major Weapon Systems Continue to Experience Cost and Schedule Problems under DOD's Revised Policy*.

Relevant support:
- GAO manufacturing-readiness work distinguishes laboratory/prototype capability from production-relevant and production-representative environments, pilot-line capability, low-rate production and full-rate production.
- GAO identified production-representative prototypes, demonstrated manufacturing processes and manufacturing/process-control evidence as important knowledge before production commitment.

Episode use:
- supports EP01-C01 and EP01-C03;
- supports the principle behind EP01-C10 that higher production commitment should be knowledge/evidence based.

Guardrail:
Do not present DoD MRL levels or milestone criteria as universal startup requirements. They are evidence that manufacturing readiness is a distinct, progressive concern, not the podcast's lifecycle taxonomy.

Sources:
https://www.gao.gov/assets/a303517.html
https://www.gao.gov/products/gao-06-368

### NIST — manufacturability/process planning during design

Primary source family:
- NIST Design and Process Planning Integration work.

Relevant support:
- conceptual process planning is described as an early-design activity for evaluating manufacturability and manufacturing cost;
- NIST identifies factors such as material, form, geometry/features, dimensions, tolerances, surface condition, production volume and production rate as relevant to manufacturing process/resource selection.

Episode use:
- supports EP01-C04;
- provides external support for the idea that manufacturing concerns should feed design before the product is effectively locked;
- supports why prototype success under one route does not answer every question about another intended production route.

Sources:
https://www.nist.gov/publications/conceptual-process-planning-definition-and-functional-decomposition
https://www.nist.gov/publications/preliminary-design-and-manufacturing-planning-integration-using-intelligent-agents

### NASA — developmental evaluation vs formal V&V / acceptance evidence

Primary source:
- NASA Systems Engineering Handbook / Product Realization and Appendix guidance.

Relevant support:
- NASA distinguishes developmental/engineering-unit evaluations from formal verification activities;
- V&V planning identifies test-article pedigree and test purpose;
- Product Verification provides objective evidence that requirements are satisfied, while Product Validation addresses intended use/environment.

Episode use:
- supports careful wording around EP01-C02: engineering/development evidence, formal product V&V, production acceptance and process-control evidence are related but should not be collapsed into one evidence class.

Sources:
https://www.nasa.gov/reference/system-engineering-handbook-appendix/
https://www.nasa.gov/reference/5-0-product-realization/
