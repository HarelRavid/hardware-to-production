## Listener transformation
Before:
“The mechanical prototype looks right and works.”

After:
“I can design the next prototype to retire explicit mechanical uncertainties in interfaces, loads, materials, tolerances, assembly and environment.”

## Two-character opportunity
Speaker B:
“If I can file or shim the prototype and it works, why treat that as a problem?”
Speaker A:
The rescue is valid learning; hiding it is the problem.

## Cold open
[ILLUSTRATIVE]
Demo works; first multi-unit build needs drilling, shimming, hand fitting, seal rework and selective assembly.

## Beat 1 — Shape vs evidence
Claim: A5-C01.
Prototype maturity = supported claims.

## Beat 2 — Separate evidence dimensions
Claim: A5-C02.
Geometry / material / surface / tolerance / joining / loads / environment.

## Beat 3 — Hand fitting is data
Claim: A5-C04.
Every unplanned rescue is evidence of uncertainty.

## Beat 4 — Interfaces and tolerance stacks
Claim: A5-C03.
Do not teach full GD&T; introduce functional contributors/CTQs only.

## Beat 5 — Material/process substitution
Claim: A5-C06.
Printed polymer, machined metal, sheet, molded part may preserve geometry but change other claims.
No numeric process rule.

## Beat 6 — Highest-value uncertainty
Claims: A5-C05/A5-C08.
The next prototype should retire consequential uncertainty, not maximize production appearance.

## Beat 7 — CTQs before final tolerance/capability
Claim: A5-C07.
Identify important characteristics without pretending final production distribution is known.

## Beat 8 — DEV / LVP / SVP
DEV: expose mechanisms quickly.
LVP: interchangeability + controlled material/process/assembly evidence.
SVP preview: released tolerance/material/process/supplier/measurement envelope.

## Listener tool execution
Sentinel enclosure:
printed shell / gasket / mounting boss / connector opening.
Record claim, failure mechanism, nonrepresentative dimension, manual rescue and next evidence.

## Misconceptions
- machined metal is automatically more serious than printed polymer;
- a tight prototype means the tolerance architecture is good;
- hand fitting is craftsmanship, not data;
- production intent means final process everywhere;
- material substitution is only a strength question.

## Closing action
Debrief the last build: list every shim, file, drill, bend, selected fit or special sequence that was not in the intended design.
