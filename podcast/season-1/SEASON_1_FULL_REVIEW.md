# Season 1 Full Review — Build the Right Thing

status: PASS WITH CONTROLLED EVIDENCE BACKLOG
scope: A1–A8 Production Blueprints

## 1. Season objective
Season 1 must transform a listener from “I have a hardware idea/prototype instinct” into a team capable of running a controlled early engineering program with explicit requirements, responsibility ownership, system interfaces, prototype evidence strategy, discipline-specific prototype maturity, verification intent and configuration identity.

Result: PASS.

## 2. Learning progression audit
Sequence:
A1 Requirements
→ A2 Ownership
→ A3 Architecture/interfaces
→ A4 Prototype representativeness
→ A5 Mechanical evidence
→ A6 Electronics/embedded evidence
→ A7 Verification planning
→ A8 Configuration/change identity

The sequence is cumulative but not mandatory-linear. Each episode can stand alone with a short recap.

Result: PASS.

## 3. Listener transformation by end of season
The listener can:
1. create a minimum useful requirement baseline;
2. map engineering responsibilities without confusing them with headcount;
3. identify high-risk subsystem interfaces and define an interface contract;
4. choose prototype technologies according to the claim they must support;
5. identify which mechanical prototype attributes are and are not representative;
6. build traceable electronics/embedded prototype identity;
7. plan tests as claim-linked verification evidence;
8. reconstruct a prototype configuration and scope change/re-verification impact.

Result: PASS — each episode adds a distinct operational capability.

## 4. Cross-episode overlap audit
### A1 vs A7
A1 owns requirements and early verification intent. A7 owns full evidence planning. Overlap is intentional and progressive.

### A2 vs A3
A2 owns responsibility coverage. A3 owns interface contracts. No problematic duplication.

### A4 vs A5/A6
A4 owns generic representativeness/shortcut-expiration logic. A5/A6 apply it to mechanical/electronics domains. Reuse is explicit.

### A7 vs A8
A7 owns what evidence must prove. A8 owns the identity/history that makes the evidence reusable. Strong paired close.

Result: PASS.

## 6. DEV/LVP/SVP continuity
Season 1 is DEV-first, but every episode previews what the shortcut becomes at LVP and what evidence becomes necessary for SVP. The season does not prematurely teach enterprise production systems.

Result: PASS.

Result: CONTROLLED GAP — not an architecture blocker.

## 8. Editorial/narrative audit
The season has a coherent story:
- define what must be true;
- ensure someone owns it;
- define where disciplines meet;
- prototype to answer questions;
- mature mechanics/electronics deliberately;
- turn testing into evidence;
- preserve the exact identity of what generated that evidence.

Recommended recurring storytelling device: Sentinel Node evolves across all eight episodes so the listener sees one product accumulate engineering discipline rather than eight disconnected examples.

Result: PASS.

## 9. Entry-point audit
Recommended entry routes:
- Idea-stage founder: A1 → full season.
- Team/ownership problem: A2 → A3 → A4.
- Existing prototype: A4 → A5/A6 → A7 → A8.
- Mechanical engineer: A5 with A4 recap.
- Electronics/embedded engineer: A6 with A3/A4 recap.
- Team preparing formal test: A7 → A8.
- Team with revision confusion: A8 standalone.

Result: PASS.

## 10. Season-level weaknesses to address during evidence/script pass
1. Avoid making A1/A2 feel management-heavy; anchor each in costly hardware failure stories.
2. A3 can become abstract; use physical interface examples early.
3. A5/A6 must remain balanced so the season does not imply mechanical/electronics are the only relevant disciplines.
4. A7 must avoid sounding like formal qualification bureaucracy; contrast lightweight DEV evidence with formal release evidence.
5. A8 must demonstrate lightweight tools before PLM terminology appears.
6. The season should explicitly invite listeners in fluidic, optical, chemical, RF or other hardware disciplines to apply the same evidence logic.
