## Why A7 + A8 are paired
A7 asks: **What exactly are we trying to prove, under which conditions, and with what evidence?**

A8 asks: **Which exact product/build/configuration did that evidence apply to, and what happens when it changes?**

Verification without configuration identity creates evidence that cannot be trusted later. Configuration control without verification intent creates bureaucracy without engineering value.

## Wave listener promise
After A7/A8, the listener should be able to:
1. turn a requirement/risk/interface into a verification claim;
2. distinguish test activity from useful evidence;
3. choose when a shortcut/test article is representative enough;
4. bind evidence to HW/BOM/FW/process/configuration identity;
5. decide what evidence must be reconsidered after a change;
6. enter later DVT/PVT/NPI content without treating those phases as magic labels.

## Shared guardrails
- Verification is claim/evidence based, not “we ran tests.”
- DVT/PVT terminology is not assumed universal across industries.
- Test success is valid only inside the demonstrated configuration/applicability envelope.
- A change does not automatically invalidate all evidence; impact follows dependencies.
- Early configuration control may be lightweight; enterprise PLM is not required in DEV.
- Rework/deviation history is preserved rather than overwritten by a final passing result.
- Standards-specific verification/CM requirements remain behind Source Verification gates.
