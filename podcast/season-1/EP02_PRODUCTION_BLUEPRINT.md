# Episode 2 Production Blueprint — POC, Prototype, MVP and Production-Intent Hardware

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 1 — Build the Right Hardware Before Production Finds Your Mistakes

## Listener transformation
The listener stops using phase labels as evidence and learns to describe build maturity dimension-by-dimension.

## Narrative hook
Two teams both say “we have an MVP.” One means a customer demo built from dev boards and printed parts; the other means a released PCB in a bridge enclosure with controlled test. Same label, radically different evidence.

## Teaching flow
1. Why build labels are useful but dangerous.
2. Difference between commercial label and engineering evidence.
3. Multidimensional maturity: function, interfaces, configuration, materials/components, process, supplier, test, reliability/compliance, rate and economics.
4. Prototype shortcut versus expiration condition.
5. Production-intent is claim-specific, not cosmetic.
6. Sentinel progression from dev-board POC to bounded LVP candidate.
7. Build Evidence Card exercise.
8. Choose the next build based on consequential evidence gaps.

## Listener tool — Build Evidence Card
Capture: build identity, objective, supported claims, representativeness, temporary shortcuts, verification evidence, open failure mechanisms, provisional production assumptions, expiration trigger and next-build objective.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: optimize learning speed. LVP: increase representativeness where the next commitment depends on it. SVP: product/process/supplier/test evidence must support the intended commercial envelope.

## Common mistakes
- Believing a cleaner enclosure means greater production maturity.
- Treating “PVT” as proof without configuration/evidence.
- Forcing every prototype dimension toward production intent too early.
- Keeping bridge technology after the evidence need has changed.

## Source/evidence backlog
External phase definitions are examples only and require named source/applicability. Internal REPRESENT/Build Evidence frameworks remain clearly labeled synthesis.

## Closing handoff
Now that the listener can describe what the build proves, Episode 3 assigns ownership for closing what it does not prove.
