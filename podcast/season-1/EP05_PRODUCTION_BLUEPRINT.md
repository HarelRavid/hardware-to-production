# Episode 5 Production Blueprint — EVT, DVT, PVT, Pilot and Ramp Without the Buzzwords

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 1 — Build the Right Hardware Before Production Finds Your Mistakes

## Listener transformation
The listener stops treating lifecycle labels as universal definitions and learns to translate every phase into explicit entry assumptions, evidence objectives and exit decisions.

## Narrative hook
A team says “we finished DVT.” Ask five people what that means and get five different answers. One means environmental testing is complete, one means final PCB, one means customer samples shipped, one means tooling started. The label created alignment only on paper.

## Teaching flow
1. Why phase labels vary across companies/industries.
2. Separate local terminology from engineering purpose.
3. Define a useful gate: purpose, entry context, product evidence, manufacturing evidence, configuration, population rationale and decision.
4. Gate Translation Table: feasibility, design verification, pilot/production-system evidence, ramp evidence, serial operation.
5. Pilot as an experiment rather than quantity milestone.
6. Sentinel “DVT” build: final-ish PCB but bridge enclosure, prototype calibration and no process capability.
7. Explain why phases can repeat/split when evidence gaps remain.

## Listener tool — Phase Evidence Card
Capture: `Local phase name → Decision after phase → Entry assumptions → Product evidence → Manufacturing evidence → Configuration → Representativeness → Population rationale → Learning/failure capture → Exit evidence → Carryover gaps`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: use phases to organize learning. LVP: connect product verification with supplier/process/test/traceability evidence. SVP/ramp: emphasize population variation, accepted throughput, recovery, economics and controlled change.

## Common mistakes
- Fixed unit count treated as proof.
- “PVT complete” used to close unresolved evidence.
- Confusing design validation with production-system validation.
- Advancing phase names while repeating the same uncertainty.

## Source/evidence backlog
External definitions of EVT/DVT/PVT/pilot/ramp remain source- and context-specific. Never state one universal definition without scoped authority.

## Closing handoff
Episodes 1–5 explained what evidence is missing and how to manage maturity. Episode 6 changes gears: how the design itself must change because manufacturing is real.
