# Episode 6 Production Blueprint — Design for Manufacturing: Designing for a Process, Not a CAD Model

status: PRODUCTION BLUEPRINT COMPLETE
season: Season 1 — Build the Right Hardware Before Production Finds Your Mistakes

## Listener transformation
The listener learns to evaluate design choices against the intended manufacturing process, its variation, measurement, tooling, supplier envelope and cost-per-good-unit.

## Narrative hook
A machined enclosure works perfectly. The team assumes the same geometry can simply be molded later. Then draft, wall thickness, shrinkage, warpage, gate location, inserts, surface requirements and tooling economics change the problem completely. Same function, different process reality.

## Teaching flow
1. DFM as product-process matching, not a generic CAD checklist.
2. Function and CTQ first; process rules second.
3. Process/material/equipment/supplier envelope.
4. Tolerance versus actual process and measurement capability.
5. Prototype-process evidence that does and does not transfer.
6. Cost per accepted good unit: yield, scrap/rework, inspection, cycle and tooling.
7. DFM Process-Design Contract.
8. Worked CNC-to-molding transition.
9. How to widen the process window through architecture rather than simply tighten control.

## Listener tool — DFM Process-Design Contract
`Function → Stage → Process → Material/state → Critical geometry → CTQ/tolerance rationale → Process capability assumption → Measurement → Tooling/access → Supplier/equipment envelope → Yield/rework risk → Economics → Bridge-to-SVP change → Evidence`.

## DEV/LVP/SVP
DEV: use fast processes while documenting nonrepresentative assumptions. LVP: bridge processes can remain if their limits and expiration triggers are explicit. SVP: design, process, supplier, measurement and economics must occupy one demonstrated envelope.

## Common mistakes
- Supplier preference presented as engineering requirement.
- Tight tolerances used as a quality reflex.
- Prototype process success treated as production-process capability.
- DFM performed after design freeze.
- Cost quoted per part while ignoring yield and inspection burden.

## Source/evidence backlog
Process-specific numerical design rules, material behavior, GD&T/GPS references and supplier/equipment limitations require verified sources and applicability. Cross-process framework remains synthesis.

## Closing handoff
DFM addresses whether the part can be produced robustly. Episode 7 asks whether the whole product can be assembled correctly without hero operators.
