# CNC Prototype Make/Buy & Supplier Selection

Provenance: GNR + industrial/government evidence to be validated per claim.

## 2.2.9.3 Make vs Buy

The decision to machine a prototype internally or externally should consider more than quoted part price.

### Key dimensions
- Required lead time and iteration frequency
- Internal spindle/machine availability
- CAM/programming capability
- Workholding/fixture capability
- Inspection capability
- Material availability
- Required tolerance and surface finish
- Confidentiality / IP sensitivity
- Number of expected design iterations
- Supplier responsiveness to engineering changes
- Need for production-like process or supplier learning
- Total landed cost including engineering and logistics

### In-house is favored when
- Learning latency is critical.
- Frequent geometry changes are expected.
- Internal capability already exists.
- Parts can be produced with standard tooling/workholding.
- Engineering wants direct process feedback.

### Outsource is favored when
- Specialized equipment or 5-axis/turn-mill capability is required.
- Material/process certification is needed.
- Internal capacity is constrained.
- Inspection requirements exceed internal metrology capability.
- Supplier capability itself is part of production-development learning.

## 2.2.9.4 Prototype Supplier Selection

Evaluate suppliers on:
- Process capability
- Material range and traceability
- Inspection/reporting capability
- Engineering communication
- Quote speed and change responsiveness
- Lead-time reliability
- Secondary operations
- Surface treatment/heat treatment network
- Document/configuration control
- Ability to support low-volume repeated builds

## Prototype supplier qualification
A prototype supplier does not always require the same qualification depth as a production supplier, but configuration control, material identity, critical-feature inspection and revision handling should be scaled to the risk of the learning objective.
