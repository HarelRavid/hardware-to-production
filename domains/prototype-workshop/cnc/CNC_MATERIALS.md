# CNC Prototype Materials

Provenance: GNR + external technical sources to be validated per claim.

## 2.2.7.1 Aluminum

### 6061-T6
Common prototype alloy because it balances machinability, strength, corrosion resistance, cost and availability. Suitable for brackets, housings, fixtures and many general mechanical prototypes.

### 7075-T6
Higher-strength aluminum with greater material cost and often higher machining/tooling burden than 6061. Useful when the prototype must evaluate higher structural loads.

### Prototype rule
Do not substitute 6061 for 7075 when the prototype is intended to validate stress margin, fastener bearing or other strength-sensitive behavior unless the difference is explicitly accounted for.

## 2.2.7.2 Carbon and Alloy Steels

Steel is appropriate when stiffness, wear, thread strength or production-material fidelity outweigh weight and machining time. Hardness and heat-treatment state strongly affect machining behavior and cost.

## 2.2.7.3 Stainless Steels

Stainless grades should be separated by family and exact grade. Austenitic stainless can work-harden and may require more conservative machining strategy than free-machining steels. Choose exact grade when corrosion, weldability, magnetic behavior or service material fidelity matters.

## 2.2.7.4 Brass and Copper

Brass is generally favorable for prototype machining and is useful for fluid, electrical and low-friction components. Copper is important when thermal/electrical conductivity is part of the prototype objective, but machinability and burr control depend strongly on grade and condition.

## 2.2.7.5 Titanium

Titanium is valuable for production-representative structural or biomedical/aerospace prototypes but typically carries high material cost and slower removal rates. Avoid using it in early form-fit prototypes unless its material behavior is relevant to the learning objective.

## 2.2.7.6 Engineering Plastics

### POM / Acetal
Useful for low-friction, dimensionally stable mechanical components, bushings, sliders and fixtures.

### PEEK
High-performance polymer for chemical, thermal and mechanical service. High material cost means it is best reserved for prototypes where service-material fidelity matters.

### PTFE
Useful for chemical resistance and low friction, but soft material behavior and dimensional instability under clamping/measurement require care.

### Polycarbonate
Tough engineering plastic useful for transparent or impact-resistant prototypes. Machining route may be preferable to printed alternatives when stock-material behavior is important.

### Acrylic / PMMA
Useful for optical/transparent demonstration parts; edge finish, cracking and stress sensitivity should be considered.

### HDPE / UHMWPE
Useful for low-friction, chemical-resistant and wear applications, but soft material behavior affects workholding and dimensional control.

## Selection dimensions

- Transparency

- Machinability
- Surface-finish requirement
- Cost and lead time
