# 3.12 Surface Engineering & Finishing — Domain Map

status: Researching

## 3.12.1 Scope
Engineering of functional and protective surface states applied before, during or after manufacturing, including mechanical finishing, chemical/electrochemical conversion, plating, anodizing, paint/powder coating, vapor-deposited and sprayed coatings, surface inspection, rework and lifecycle effects.

## 3.12.3 Functional objectives
- corrosion protection
- wear resistance
- friction control
- electrical conductivity/insulation
- thermal behavior
- adhesion promotion
- cosmetic appearance
- sealing/surface smoothness
- reflectivity/optical boundary
- biocompatibility/cleanability boundary

## 3.12.4 Core engineering questions
1. What functional requirement is the finish solving?
2. What substrate and pretreatment state are required?
3. Does coating thickness affect dimensions, threads, fits or sealing surfaces?
4. What masking/racking/contact strategy is needed?
5. Can the coating reach internal/complex features uniformly?
6. How is adhesion, coverage and porosity verified?
7. What process/environmental risks arise during stripping or rework?
8. Does the coating remain compatible with downstream joining, sealing, cleaning and service environment?

## 3.12.5 Master decision object
### D-SURF-MASTER-001 — Which surface-engineering route should be used?
Inputs: substrate, functional requirement, environment, geometry, internal features, tolerances/GD&T, finish/surface condition, appearance, electrical/thermal needs, downstream operations, volume, automation, inspection, repair/rework and lifecycle cost.

Output: candidate process routes + pretreatment + masking/racking + inspection + trade-offs + evidence + assumptions/open questions.

## 3.12.7 Integrity rule
A coating name alone does not define performance. Substrate, preparation, coating process, thickness, post-treatment, test method and service environment belong to the evidence scope. AI-created synthesis remains GNR until evidence-linked.
