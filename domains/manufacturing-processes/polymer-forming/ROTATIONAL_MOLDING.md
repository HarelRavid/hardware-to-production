## 3.7.12.2 DFM Principles
Rotational molding is suited to hollow, often relatively large polymer parts made without the high injection pressures of injection molding. The design challenge is controlled material distribution during heating/rotation and stable cooling.

Key questions:
- Does the geometry benefit from a seamless hollow construction?
- Are wall-thickness variation and corner accumulation acceptable?
- Can inserts, openings and interfaces be positioned without destabilizing material distribution?
- Can the required dimensional tolerance be achieved directly or should interfaces be machined afterward?

## 3.7.12.3 Failure / Variation Objects
- uneven wall thickness
- incomplete fusion
- bubbles / porosity
- warpage
- corner accumulation
- surface defects
- insert movement
- dimensional drift
- long cooling-cycle sensitivity

## 3.7.12.5 Economics
Drivers:
- mold cost
- long cycle time
- cavity count / machine-arm utilization
- part size
- labor and demolding
- trimming/secondary machining
- material charge and scrap

## 3.7.12.6 Why Projects Fail
- expecting injection-molding-like dimensional precision
- ignoring cooling-driven warpage
- placing functional interfaces without a machining/inspection strategy
- prototype made by fabrication gives false confidence in production wall distribution
- inserts added without validating thermal and positional behavior
