# 3.7 Polymer Forming Beyond Injection Molding

status: Researching
provenance: [GNR]

## 3.7.1 Domain Purpose
This domain covers polymer-forming processes other than conventional injection molding, with emphasis on DFM, tooling, material behavior, process capability, production economics, automation, quality and transition from development builds to serial production.

## 3.7.2 Process Taxonomy
- Profile / sheet / film extrusion
- Extrusion blow molding
- Injection blow molding
- Stretch blow molding
- Thermoforming
- Vacuum forming
- Pressure forming
- Rotational molding
- Compression molding
- Transfer molding
- Reaction injection molding (RIM)
- Pultrusion / continuous polymer-composite forming (cross-linked to composites)

## 3.7.3 Cross-cutting Engineering Modules
- Polymer rheology and melt behavior
- Thermal history and cooling
- Shrinkage and dimensional stability
- Orientation and anisotropy
- Wall-thickness control
- Tooling and mold/die design
- Trimming and secondary operations
- Material drying / conditioning
- Regrind and material reuse
- Surface / cosmetic quality
- Process windows and variation
- Automation and handling
- Inspection and capability
- Tooling economics and production volume
- Sustainability and material yield

## 3.7.4 Questions This Domain Must Answer
1. When should extrusion, blow molding, thermoforming, rotational molding, compression molding or RIM be considered instead of injection molding or machining?
2. Which processes inherently constrain geometry to a constant section, hollow body or formed sheet?
3. How do wall-thickness distribution, draw ratio, parison behavior and shrinkage influence part design?
4. Which properties of the production part may differ from a prototype made by additive manufacturing or machining?
5. How do tooling cost, cycle time, automation and expected volume shift the preferred process?
6. Which secondary operations—trimming, drilling, welding, printing, machining—must be planned as part of the primary process?
7. What must be validated before a prototype process becomes a repeatable serial-production process?

## 3.7.5 Lifecycle
