## 3.7.13.4 DFM Principles
These processes require process-specific thinking rather than treating them as variants of thermoplastic injection molding. Material reaction/cure, charge placement, fiber/filler behavior, flow, venting, compression and mold thermal control can dominate the design.

Questions:
- Does the selected material require a reactive/cure process?
- Is fiber reinforcement or high filler loading a key reason to select compression molding?
- Are large relatively low-pressure molded structures better suited to RIM than conventional injection molding?
- Which inserts/features can be molded in reliably?
- How will cure state and material mixing be verified?

## 3.7.13.7 Production Engineering
- material batch control
- storage/conditioning
- charge weight and placement
- mold temperature
- pressure/force profile
- cure time
- mix ratio where applicable
- automated material delivery
- trim/flash removal
- cure verification

## 3.7.13.8 Why Projects Fail
- assuming thermoset/reactive material behavior follows thermoplastic injection-molding rules
- ignoring cure kinetics in cycle-time estimates
- poor control of charge placement or mixing
- production tooling designed before material/process window is mature
- prototype property data used without matching production cure/fiber/process state
