## 3.7.10.3 DFM Principles
Blow molding is primarily a hollow-part process. The design problem is therefore not only exterior geometry but controlled material distribution around a closed or semi-closed volume.

Key questions:
- Does the product function justify a hollow-body process?
- How will wall thickness vary around shoulders, corners, handles and pinch-off regions?
- Which interfaces require injection-molded precision, inserts or secondary machining?
- How will the product be supported, trimmed and leak-tested?

## 3.7.10.4 Variation / Failure Objects

- incomplete inflation
- pinch-off weakness
- flash

- warpage

- leaks

## 3.7.10.7 Why Projects Fail
- assuming nominal CAD wall thickness can be reproduced uniformly
- ignoring pinch-off and trim geometry
- using a prototype method that does not reveal stretch/thickness distribution risks
- specifying critical sealing interfaces without process-specific control strategy
- postponing leak-test planning until pilot production
