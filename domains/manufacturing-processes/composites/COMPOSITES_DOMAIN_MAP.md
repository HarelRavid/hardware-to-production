# 3.8 Composite Manufacturing — Domain Map

status: Researching
provenance: [GNR]

## 3.8.1 Scope
Manufacturing engineering of fiber-reinforced polymer composite parts and structures from development through repeatable serial production.

## 3.8.2 Process families
- Hand layup / wet layup
- Vacuum bagging
- Vacuum infusion / VARTM
- RTM / light RTM
- Prepreg layup
- Autoclave cure
- Out-of-autoclave prepreg
- Filament winding
- Pultrusion
- Compression molding / SMC / BMC
- Automated tape laying / automated fiber placement
- Composite machining and trimming
- Bonded/sandwich assembly

## 3.8.3 Material-system objects
- Fiber: carbon, glass, aramid, basalt, specialty
- Matrix: epoxy, polyester, vinyl ester, thermoplastic matrices
- Reinforcement form: UD, woven, braid, mat, chopped fiber, prepreg
- Core: foam, honeycomb, balsa and other sandwich cores
- Resin content / fiber volume fraction
- Ply orientation and stacking sequence
- Cure system

## 3.8.4 Cross-cutting modules
1. Laminate architecture and manufacturability
2. Resin flow / impregnation
3. Cure kinetics and thermal management
4. Tooling and dimensional control
5. Vacuum systems and consumables
6. Defects and process-induced damage
7. Inspection / NDT
8. Joining and inserts
9. Trimming / drilling / machining
10. Automation
11. Production economics
12. Qualification and traceability
13. Repair and lifecycle

## 3.8.5 Core engineering questions
- Which composite process fits geometry, laminate, volume and performance?
- Is the prototype process representative of serial production?
- Which laminate requirements create manufacturing risk?
- How are resin flow, voids and cure controlled?
- When is autoclave processing justified?
- How should tooling account for cure temperature, CTE and spring-in/spring-back?
- Which defects are detectable and which require process prevention?
- How should holes, inserts and bonded joints be designed for production?
- When should manual layup transition to AFP/ATL, RTM, compression molding or another process?

## 3.8.6 Master decision object
### D-COMP-MASTER-001 — Which composite manufacturing route should be used?
Inputs: structural requirement, laminate architecture, geometry, dimensions, surface requirement, fiber/matrix, volume, takt, tooling budget, cure constraints, inspection, traceability, repair, supplier capability and lifecycle.

Output: candidate routes + trade-offs + evidence + assumptions + open questions.

## 3.8.7 Knowledge integrity
Quantitative rules, defect thresholds, cure windows, laminate allowables and process capability are not universalized. AI-created synthesis remains GNR until evidence-linked.
