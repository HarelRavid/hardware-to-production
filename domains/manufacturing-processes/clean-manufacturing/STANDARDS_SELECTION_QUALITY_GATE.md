## Standards/source architecture to populate
Primary families to research and scope:
- ISO 16232 road-vehicle component cleanliness family and current revision/applicability
- VDA 19 technical-cleanliness guidance and boundaries
- ISO 14644 cleanroom/controlled-environment family where applicable
- ASTM/ISO surface-cleanliness and residue test methods where relevant
- application-specific oxygen-service, vacuum, hydraulic, medical, semiconductor/electronics, food/pharma or other precision-cleaning requirements where applicable
- water-quality and analytical methods used by the selected cleanliness requirement

A standard from one industry is not automatically normative for another. Cross-industry use must be labeled as informative/analogical unless the application adopts it.

- EHS/environmental constraints

- total cost of contamination

## Integrity gates before Podcast Ready
1. “Clean” always has a defined contaminant/measurement context.
2. Visual cleanliness is not substituted for required quantitative verification.
3. Sampling/extraction is part of the measurement system.
4. Cleaning chemistry is checked against every relevant material in the part/assembly.
5. Drainage/drying of internal geometry is addressed.
6. Verified cleanliness is preserved through handling and packaging.
7. Environmental classification is not treated as proof of product cleanliness.
8. Cross-industry standards are not presented as normative without applicability.
9. Decisions expose assumptions/open questions.
10. GNR remains visible until verified.
