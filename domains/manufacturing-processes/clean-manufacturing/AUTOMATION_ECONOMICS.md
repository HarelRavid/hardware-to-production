- recipe control
- barcode/serial genealogy

## Cost architecture
Cost per verified-clean part may include:
- cleaning chemistry
- water/high-purity water
- energy/heating
- machine/bath occupancy
- labor
- filtration
- drying
- environmental controls
- waste treatment/disposal
- verification/testing
- controlled handling
- clean packaging
- re-cleaning
- scrap/rework caused by contamination
- downtime/bath maintenance

## Hidden-cost objects
- contamination-caused coating/bond failures
- blocked fluid passages
- premature wear
- leak/seal failures
- test failures
- field reliability
- repeated cleaning because preservation failed

## Decision objects
### D-CLEAN-AUTO-001 — Manual, batch or automated cleaning line?
### D-CLEAN-MAKEBUY-001 — Internal cleaning or qualified external service?
### D-CLEAN-ECON-001 — Prevention investment vs downstream cleaning/quality cost

## Engineering principle
Cleaning economics must include the cost of contamination-induced failures and preservation, not only the direct cost of the washer or chemistry.

No universal automation-volume threshold is asserted.
