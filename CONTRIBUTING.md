# Contributing

## Purpose

Contributions should turn scattered information into traceable, reusable engineering knowledge for the transition from hardware development to scalable production.

## Before adding a source

1. Check whether it already exists in `SOURCE_INDEX.csv`.
2. Prefer the latest official edition or primary publication.
3. Record the exact title, author or organization, year, language, access type, and URL.
4. State the industry and lifecycle stage to which the source applies.
5. Do not present vendor guidance as a universal requirement.

## Source status

Use one of the following values:

- `Discovered` — identified but not yet reviewed
- `Screened` — relevance checked
- `Read` — relevant sections reviewed
- `Summarized` — structured notes completed
- `Verified` — claims checked against the original source and edition
- `Superseded` — replaced by a newer edition or source
- `Rejected` — unreliable, irrelevant, or duplicative

## Source notes template

Each substantial source summary should contain:

```markdown
# Source title

## Bibliographic details
## Why this source matters
## Scope and limitations
## Key concepts
## Actionable conclusions
## Relevant lifecycle stages
## Relevant industries
## Podcast applications
## Quotable passages to verify
## Related sources
## Review status
```

## Standards and copyrighted material

Do not upload copyrighted standards, books, paid course materials, or restricted publications. Store only bibliographic details, lawful links, edition information, original summaries, and short quotations where permitted.

## Evidence discipline

Clearly distinguish:

- Legal or regulatory obligation
- Normative standard requirement
- Industry-specific customer requirement
- Recommended practice
- Academic finding
- Manufacturer recommendation
- Practitioner opinion

## File naming

Use lowercase `snake_case` for folders and Markdown files. Use descriptive names rather than unexplained abbreviations.

## Change quality

A useful contribution should improve at least one of the following:

- Traceability to evidence
- Clarity of a manufacturing decision
- Readiness-gate definition
- Reusable checklist, matrix, or template
- Connection between product design and production outcome
- Podcast structure or explanation quality
