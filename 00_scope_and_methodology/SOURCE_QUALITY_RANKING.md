# Source Quality Ranking

## Level A — Binding or authoritative primary source

Examples:

- Law or regulation
- Regulator publication
- Official standard or normative industry requirement
- Contractual customer-specific requirement

Use for obligations, definitions, required controls, and compliance claims. Applicability must still be established.

## Level B — High-authority technical source

Examples:

- Peer-reviewed academic paper
- Government or national-laboratory technical report
- Official handbook from a recognized technical body
- Accredited university course material based on established literature

Use for technical mechanisms, validated methods, and evidence-based comparison.

## Level C — Recognized professional guidance

Examples:

- Established engineering textbook
- Industry-association manual
- Professional certification body material
- Widely adopted technical reference

Use for structured methods, terminology, implementation frameworks, and teaching.

## Level D — Applied industry source

Examples:

- Manufacturer application guide
- Contract manufacturer or automation integrator guide
- Engineering consultancy paper
- Industrial conference presentation

Use for practical implementation details, examples, and emerging practice. Check commercial bias and scope.

## Level E — Practitioner or informal source

Examples:

- Blog
- Podcast
- Forum discussion
- Personal case study
- Unsourced training notes

Use for leads, operational questions, and examples. Do not rely on it alone for critical technical or compliance conclusions.

## Evaluation dimensions

Each source should also be evaluated for:

- Relevance to the exact decision
- Recency and edition
- Traceability to evidence
- Industry applicability
- Production-volume applicability
- Independence and commercial bias
- Clarity of assumptions
- Reproducibility
- Availability of the original source

## Citation confidence

- **Confirmed** — statement checked directly in the cited source
- **Supported** — source supports the conclusion, but synthesis or interpretation is involved
- **Indicative** — useful lead requiring stronger confirmation
- **Unverified** — not suitable for publication or recording
