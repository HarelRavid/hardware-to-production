# Research Method

## 1. Research objective

Build an evidence-based, practical knowledge system for moving hardware from a mature proof of concept or MVP into initial production, pilot production, ramp-up, and mass production.

## 2. Research questions

For every topic, the research should answer:

1. What decision must the team make?
2. At which lifecycle stage must it be made?
3. Which function owns the decision?
4. What evidence or deliverable is required?
5. What commonly fails when the decision is delayed or skipped?
6. How does the answer change with product type, industry, production volume, and regulation?
7. What can be converted into a checklist, gate, template, or podcast explanation?

## 3. Search layers

Research will be performed in descending order of authority:

1. Laws, regulations, and regulators
2. Standards and official guidance
3. Academic papers and recognized technical handbooks
4. University courses and institutional teaching material
5. Industry associations and sector manuals
6. Manufacturer and contract-manufacturer guides
7. Practitioner case studies, conference talks, podcasts, and blogs

Lower-level sources may provide useful operational insight but must not be used to invent universal requirements.

## 4. Source screening

A source is retained only when it contributes to one or more of the following:

- Lifecycle or readiness definition
- Engineering decision criteria
- Required deliverable or review gate
- Manufacturing-process selection
- Quality, reliability, test, or validation method
- Supply-chain or cost decision
- Automation strategy
- Pilot or ramp-up practice
- Case evidence of success or failure

## 5. Extraction format

For every retained source, capture:

- Bibliographic details and stable link
- Edition or revision
- Source authority
- Scope and exclusions
- Industry and production-volume context
- Lifecycle stages addressed
- Key conclusions
- Relevant tables, figures, clauses, or chapters
- Limitations and conflicting evidence
- Reusable outputs
- Potential podcast applications

## 6. Verification rules

- Verify claims against the original source whenever possible.
- Record the exact edition of standards and official documents.
- Mark secondary summaries as secondary.
- Do not infer legal applicability without jurisdiction and product context.
- Separate a source’s statement from our interpretation.
- Recheck links and current editions before publication or recording.

## 7. Synthesis method

Sources will be synthesized into:

- Lifecycle maps
- Manufacturing-readiness matrices
- Role and responsibility maps
- Gate-entry and exit criteria
- Decision trees
- DFX checklists
- Process-selection guides
- Automation business-case models
- Pilot and ramp dashboards
- Failure case studies
- Podcast episode briefs

## 8. Conflict handling

When sources disagree:

1. Check whether they address different industries or volumes.
2. Check edition dates and terminology.
3. Prefer mandatory or primary sources for factual obligations.
4. Preserve legitimate alternative practices.
5. State the assumptions under which each practice is valid.

## 9. Review status

Every major page should display one of these states:

- Draft
- Source collection
- Technical review required
- Verified
- Superseded

## 10. Initial research streams

1. Product and manufacturing maturity models
2. NPI and design transfer
3. EVT, DVT, PVT, pilot, and ramp definitions
4. DFM, DFA, DFT, DFR, DFS, and design for automation
5. Manufacturing-process families
6. Tooling, fixtures, and production test
7. Quality planning and reliability
8. Supplier and contract-manufacturer readiness
9. Documentation and configuration control
10. MES, ERP, PLM, automation, and traceability
11. Production economics and capacity
12. Standards and sector-specific requirements
13. Hardware startup and industrial case studies
14. Hebrew and Israeli sources
