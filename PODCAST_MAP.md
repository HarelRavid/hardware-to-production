# Podcast Map

## Working premise

A working hardware product is not yet a production-ready product. The podcast will explain the operational, engineering, quality, supply-chain, and organizational changes required to cross that gap.

## Audience

Primary:

- Hardware development engineers
- NPI and industrialization engineers
- Manufacturing and process engineers
- Technical founders and engineering managers

Secondary:

- Quality, reliability, test, supply-chain, and operations teams
- Product managers working with physical products
- Students transitioning into industrial hardware roles

## Proposed season architecture

### Part I — Understanding the gap

1. **The product works. Why can’t we manufacture it?**
2. **POC, prototype, MVP, and production-intent hardware**
3. **What NPI and industrialization actually own**
4. **Why product readiness and manufacturing readiness are different**

### Part II — Designing for production

5. **EVT, DVT, PVT, pilot, and ramp without the buzzwords**
6. **Design for manufacturing**
7. **Design for assembly and mistake prevention**
8. **Design for test, calibration, and traceability**
9. **Tolerance, variation, and why one good prototype proves little**
10. **Designing for reliability, service, and repair**

### Part III — Building the production system

11. **From EBOM to MBOM and process flow**
12. **Selecting manufacturing processes and suppliers**
13. **Tooling, jigs, fixtures, and production-intent equipment**
14. **Work instructions, training, and operator qualification**
15. **DFMEA, PFMEA, control plans, and quality gates**
16. **Production testing and measurement-system capability**

### Part IV — Pilot and ramp

17. **How to plan a pilot build**
18. **What a production validation build must prove**
19. **Yield, rework, scrap, and the learning curve**
20. **Capacity, bottlenecks, takt time, and line balance**
21. **Supplier readiness and contract-manufacturer management**
22. **Engineering changes during ramp-up**

### Part V — Automation and scale

23. **When not to automate**
24. **Building the business case for automation**
25. **Semi-automation, robotics, machine vision, and end-of-line test**
26. **PLM, ERP, MES, QMS, and the digital thread**
27. **Traceability and production data that engineers can actually use**
28. **The organizational transition to mass production**

### Part VI — Case studies

29. **Hardware launch failures and what was missed**
30. **Successful industrialization patterns**
31. **Startup constraints: speed without manufacturing debt**
32. **Lessons from automotive, medical, aerospace, and industrial equipment**

## Episode research brief

Each episode should eventually contain:

1. The practical problem
2. A representative failure scenario
3. Definitions and boundaries
4. What changes between development and production
5. Roles and ownership
6. Required deliverables
7. Decision criteria
8. Applicable standards and evidence
9. Common failure modes
10. A practical checklist
11. Guest profile and interview questions
12. Sources for show notes

## Research-to-episode workflow

`Source discovered → screened → summarized → synthesized into tool or framework → assigned to episode → technically reviewed → recorded`

## Initial anchor deliverable

The first cross-episode framework will be a **Hardware Manufacturing Readiness Matrix** covering:

- Product definition
- Design maturity
- BOM maturity
- Supplier maturity
- Tooling maturity
- Process maturity
- Test maturity
- Quality maturity
- Documentation maturity
- Automation maturity
- Capacity maturity
- Cost maturity
- Service maturity
- Compliance maturity
- Organizational readiness
