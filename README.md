# Hardware to Production

> A practical knowledge base for hardware NPI, industrialization, manufacturing methods, automation, pilot production, ramp-up, and scalable manufacturing.

## Mission

This repository helps hardware development and manufacturing teams turn a working proof of concept or mature MVP into a repeatable, testable, compliant, economical, and scalable product.

The central question is not only **“Does the product work?”** but:

- Can it be manufactured repeatedly?
- Can it be assembled and tested consistently?
- Can suppliers reproduce the required quality?
- Can changes be controlled?
- Can production scale without losing yield, reliability, traceability, or margin?

## Intended audience

- Hardware and systems engineers
- Mechanical, electrical, electronics, firmware, and test engineers
- NPI and industrialization engineers
- Manufacturing and process engineers
- Quality and reliability engineers
- Supply-chain and operations teams
- Technical founders and product leaders

## Lifecycle covered

`Advanced POC → Engineering Prototype → EVT → DVT → PVT → Pilot Production → Ramp-up → Mass Production`

Terminology differs across industries. This repository focuses on the evidence, deliverables, decisions, risks, and exit criteria behind each stage rather than treating stage names as universal.

## Knowledge map

| Area | Core question |
|---|---|
| Product maturity | Is the design stable and fully defined? |
| NPI and industrialization | Is ownership clear and is the transfer controlled? |
| Design for X | Can the product be manufactured, assembled, tested, serviced, and scaled? |
| Manufacturing processes | Which process, material, tooling, and joining method fit the product and volume? |
| Production-system design | What flow, layout, capacity, takt time, and staffing are required? |
| Tooling and test | Can production build and verify every unit consistently? |
| Quality and reliability | Are risks, controls, measurement systems, and validation adequate? |
| Supply chain | Are suppliers, lead times, alternates, costs, and obsolescence controlled? |
| Documentation and configuration | Are EBOM, MBOM, drawings, instructions, and changes synchronized? |
| Automation | What should be automated, when, and with what business case? |
| Pilot, ramp, and mass production | Can output grow while yield, cost, and quality remain controlled? |
| Standards and compliance | Which management, product, safety, and sector standards apply? |

## Working principles

1. **Primary sources first.** Standards, regulators, academic papers, official handbooks, and recognized industry bodies take precedence.
2. **Separate obligation from guidance.** A legal requirement, normative standard clause, recommended practice, and vendor opinion are not equivalent.
3. **Preserve context.** Every conclusion must state its product type, production volume, industry, lifecycle stage, and assumptions.
4. **Do not confuse prototype success with production readiness.** Product, process, test, supplier, quality, and organizational readiness are assessed separately.
5. **Prefer reusable outputs.** Research should result in checklists, decision matrices, templates, gate criteria, and podcast-ready explanations.
6. **Control versions.** Standards, links, source dates, and document revisions must be recorded and periodically reverified.
