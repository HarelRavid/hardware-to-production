## Purpose
Build a defensible case-study layer for the final podcast arc. Cases are used to teach transferable decisions, not to decorate episodes with famous anecdotes.

## Case evidence hierarchy
Prefer:
1. regulator/government investigation or official technical report;
2. company technical/postmortem material with concrete evidence;
3. peer-reviewed or authoritative case study;
4. high-quality industry analysis with named evidence;
5. secondary reporting only when primary evidence is unavailable and uncertainty is explicit.

Avoid unsourced manufacturing folklore.

## Case object template
Each case must capture:
- case ID/title;
- product/company/industry;
- source quality;
- documented facts;
- lifecycle stage at failure/success;
- relevant Hardware Evolution Ladder transition;
- DEV/LVP/SVP context;
- technical/manufacturing mechanism;
- organizational/system mechanism;
- detection/escape mechanism;
- consequence;
- corrective action where documented;
- transferable lesson;
- limits of transfer;
- linked A0–A8 canonical claims;
- episode mapping;
- unresolved questions.

## Episodes 56–59 — Cross-industry lessons
For each industry, extract both useful practices and transfer limits.

### Automotive
Likely themes: APQP/PPAP, supplier quality, high-rate capability, mistake-proofing, standardized work, change discipline.
Transfer limit: high volume and mature supply networks can make some practices economically inappropriate for DEV/LVP startups.

### Medical devices
Likely themes: risk management, design controls, process validation, traceability, change/document control.
Transfer limit: regulatory obligations are product/jurisdiction specific and must not be generalized to all hardware.

### Aerospace
Likely themes: configuration management, FAI, special processes, supplier oversight, traceability, low-volume/high-consequence production.
Transfer limit: assurance burden and economics differ sharply from consumer/industrial products.

### Industrial equipment
Likely themes: maintainability, long lifecycle, field service, spares, configuration variants, machinery safety, integration with customer sites.
Transfer limit: project/engineer-to-order models differ from repeat high-volume production.

## Episode 60 — Full Decision Story
Build one composite/fictitious product journey. The story may be fictional, but every engineering rule used in it must map to evidence-backed A0–A9 claims.

Required transitions:
Idea → Requirements → Architecture → POC → Integrated Prototype → Engineering Prototype → Production Intent → LVP → Production Validation → Ramp → SVP.

At each transition show:
- what evidence exists;
- what remains uncertain;
- what shortcut is acceptable;
- what shortcut expires;
- what decision is made;
- what would change at a different volume/risk context.

## Initial evidence lead
NIST manufacturing traceability work is a useful example of authoritative manufacturing-data guidance: it emphasizes traceability/trustworthiness of manufacturing-related data and standards-based representations. It can support cases involving data integrity and traceability but should not be stretched into unrelated product-quality claims.
